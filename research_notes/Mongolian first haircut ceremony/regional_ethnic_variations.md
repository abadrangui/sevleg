# Regional and ethnic variations of the Mongolian child's first haircut ceremony (Үсний найр / Сэвлэг үргээх / Даахь үргээх)

**In short:** Good, specific sources exist for only a few groups: Buryat (Tunka, Russia), Kalmyk, the Oirat-descended Deed/Haixi Mongols in Qinghai, the Tuvans of China, and Kazakhs (but not Kazakhs *in Mongolia*). For most groups inside Mongolia (Torguud, Zakhchin, Ööld, Altai Uriankhai, Khoton, Darkhad, Tsaatan, Dariganga, Üzemchin, Barga, Khamnigan, Sartuul, Mongolian Tuvans), I found **no** group-specific description online. The one ethnographic text that names groups puts Khalkh, Dörvöd, Bayad, Myangad and Khotgoid in **one** pattern (elder or grandfather cuts first) and gives the Buryat a different one (the woman who delivered the baby cuts first). The clearest pattern across groups is **who cuts first**: an elder or grandfather (Khalkh and most Oirat in Mongolia), the **maternal uncle** (Kalmyk, Tunka Buryat, Tuvans of China), the **midwife** (Buryat in older Mongolian sources), or an **elder whose zodiac sign matches the child's** (Haixi/Deed Mongols, Inner Mongolia).

Confidence key used below:
- **[High]**: three or more independent sources agree.
- **[Medium]**: one solid source (ethnography, a heritage register, a dissertation, a recorded interview), or two weaker sources that agree.
- **[Low]**: one weak source (blog, ad, tourist site), a search-result snippet only, or wording that is unclear.

## 1. Summary comparison table (table-ready facts per group)

### Takeaway
The biggest differences between groups are in (a) **age** (40 days for Kazakhs, 1 year for Kalmyks and Tunka Buryats, 3 years for Haixi Mongols, 2–5 years for Khalkh), (b) **who cuts first**, and (c) **what is done with the cut hair**. Most groups use scissors tied with a khadag (silk scarf), say blessings, and give the child livestock or money.

### Inferences
- There is a clear "maternal uncle cuts first" cluster: Kalmyk, Tunka Buryat, and Tuvans of China. In standard Khalkh practice the father's side or the eldest guest leads. This difference is worth a separate column in the final table.
- Oirat-descended groups *outside* Mongolia (Haixi/Deed Mongols, Subei Mongols in Gansu) keep a strong, documented version with long blessing poems, sunrise timing and a zodiac-matched first cutter. This may be the best documented "Oirat version", but it should not be assumed to match Dörvöd or Torguud practice inside Mongolia.

### Gaps
- No group-specific source was found online for Torguud, Zakhchin, Ööld, Altai Uriankhai, Khoton, Darkhad, Tsaatan/Dukha, Dariganga, Üzemchin, Barga, Khamnigan, Sartuul, or the Tuvans of Mongolia (Tsengel). The printed ethnographies by С. Бадамхатан and others are probably needed; they are not online in searchable form.

## 2. Khalkh: the "standard" version, and differences between central, Gobi, eastern and western Khalkh areas

### Takeaway
Many Mongolian sources say the rite "differs from place to place", but almost none say *how* for named Khalkh sub-areas. The few concrete regional details are: who cuts first (grandfather/father vs the person whose zodiac year protects the child), a Thursday taboo "in some areas", Khalkh seasonal and day preferences, and a separate hairstyle custom in the "western aimags".

### Cited Findings
- General statement that the custom varies by area: "Хүүхдийн үс авах ёс нь нутаг нутагт арай өөр өөр заншилтай." [High] — [Zindaa 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); also [arkhangai.mn 2018](http://arkhangai.mn/index.php?newsid=3616) ("нутаг нутгийн онцлогоос хамаарч"); [mongolmom 2016](https://mongolmom.blogspot.com/2016/04/mongolian-childrens-first-hair-cutting.html) ("varies from each Mongolian region to another").
- Who cuts first varies by place: in some places the grandfather or father starts; in others the person whose zodiac year protects the child (ивээл жилтэй хүн) touches the hair first. Cutting starts on the right side. [Medium] — [Zindaa 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn).
- Khalkh timing preference: mid-summer or autumn, on the 8th or 15th lunar day, on a Monday or Friday; Thursday is avoided as "хүний хишиг өдөр". [Medium] — [PDFCOFFEE (TANiiNOM.mn book text)](https://pdfcoffee.com/--14601-pdf-free.html).
- "Some areas" do not cut on the 4th day of the week (Thursday): "дөрөв дэх өдөр хүүхдийн даахийг авдаггүй байсан". It is linked to "хүний хишиг өдөр". Ethnologist С. Дулам is quoted saying this was done "in some areas" (зарим нутагт), not everywhere. [Medium] — [Zindaa 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); headline of [tusgal.mn interview with С. Дулам](http://tusgal.mn/s-dulam-khuukhdiin-daakhiig-purew-garagt-awdaggui-baisan) (site was down; known from search results only).
- **Western aimags:** girls keep a lock called **сонжуу**; boys keep three fingers' width of hair on the forehead, called **согсоо**. The source says this custom is "almost abandoned and forgotten". One reposted version adds that a girl's сонжуу grows thicker and at 14–16 she starts wearing "тав гэзэг" / "гол үс". [Medium] — [Zindaa 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); same text appears on [asu.mn](http://asu.mn/content/2018) and [zogii.mn](https://zogii.mn/newsm/8/2/157196) (search results).
- Standard Khalkh shape of the hair left: boys keep a tuft (тэвэг), girls two locks (хөхөл). This is said to stand for the future household head and mother. "Recently" girls often get only a token trim of the braid. [High] — [mn.wikipedia talk page (Гаадамба/Хорлоо text)](https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A5%D1%8D%D1%80%D1%8D%D0%B3%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3%D1%87%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD_%D1%8F%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B0:Pimp.at.mn); [PDFCOFFEE](https://pdfcoffee.com/--14601-pdf-free.html).
- Hair is also said to be used for healing ("эмчилгээнд хэрэг болно"), and loose hair should not be left lying around (it "shortens life and drains fortune"). [Low–Medium] — [Zindaa 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn).
- Another account says the khadag with the hair is kept by the family and given to the child in the late pre-teen or early teenage years. [Low] — [Val Farmer blog, 2014](https://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html?m=1).

### Inferences
- The "western aimags" сонжуу/согсоо custom may belong to Oirat groups (Dörvöd, Torguud, Zakhchin, etc.) rather than to western Khalkh. The source does not name groups, so this is unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- No source compares central, Gobi, and eastern Khalkh areas directly. I found no aimag or soum (district) website that describes a local variant.
- I could not open the С. Дулам interview (tusgal.mn was down) or mongolcom.mn articles 9427 and 8394 (site not reachable). They may hold more regional detail.

## 3. Western Oirat groups: Дөрвөд, Торгууд, Захчин, Өөлд, Баяд, Altai Урианхай, Мянгад, Хотон

### Takeaway
Inside Mongolia, the only named Oirat-related groups in an ethnographic text (Dörvöd, Bayad, Myangad, and Khotgoid, who are near-Oirat) follow the **same** first-cutter rule as Khalkh. I found **nothing** specific for Torguud, Zakhchin, Ööld, Altai Uriankhai or Khoton. Outside Mongolia, the **Deed/Haixi Mongols of Qinghai (Khoshut Oirat)** and the **Subei Mongols (Gansu)** have a well-documented Oirat version called "Orbo" (敖尔波).

### Cited Findings
- "Халх, дөрвөд, баяд, мянгад, хотгойд ястанд хүүхдийн үс авахад өвөг эцэг, эцэг нь эсвэл тухайн ёслолд оролцож буй хамгийн өндөр настай хүндтэй хүн" (the grandfather, the father, or the oldest respected person present cuts first). The text cites Гаадамба (1968, 1978), Гаадамба & Цэрэнсодном (1978), and Хорлоо (1969). [Medium] — [mn.wikipedia talk page](https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A5%D1%8D%D1%80%D1%8D%D0%B3%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3%D1%87%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD_%D1%8F%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B0:Pimp.at.mn); same text in [PDFCOFFEE / TANiiNOM.mn](https://pdfcoffee.com/--14601-pdf-free.html).
- Haixi Mongols are the main body of the **Khoshut**, one of the four Oirat tribes. They are also called "Deed Mongols" (德都蒙古). [Medium–High] — [ihchina.cn, 邸莎若拉, 2023-08-28](https://www.ihchina.cn/luntan_details/27930.html); [Baidu (Russian)](https://baike.baidu.com/ru/item/%D0%9E%D0%B1%D1%80%D1%8F%D0%B4%20%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B6%D0%BA%D0%B8%20%D0%B2%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%BE%D1%81%20%D1%85%D0%B0%D0%B9%D1%81%D0%B8-%D0%BC%D0%BE%D0%BD%D0%B3%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%B2/857888).
- Haixi/Deed rite details from fieldwork at a 2021 ceremony:
  - Hair is not cut from birth until **age 3**.
  - **A lama does a divination** to choose the date.
  - The order is first cut, then family, then guests.
  - The first cut must happen **just as the sun rises**, made by an elder whose **zodiac sign matches** the child's. Boys are cut from the right side, girls from the left.
  - The cut lock goes into the **khadag knot on the scissor handle**.
  - Khadag, silk and felt rugs are draped over the child, and the first cutter promises **a little white ewe lamb**.
  - Each person must **say a blessing aloud**; if they cannot, someone else says it for them.
  - Today, girls get less hair cut so it can be styled; boys are usually **shaved completely** after the rite.

  [Medium–High] — [ihchina.cn, 2023](https://www.ihchina.cn/luntan_details/27930.html).
- Haixi feast: whole sheep (德都蒙古全席, Deed Mongol whole-sheep banquet), "дээж" and "баянсүн" offerings, Deed folk songs, and horse-head fiddle (morin khuur). On the first Qinghai provincial intangible heritage list in 2006 (number Ⅹ-6). [Medium–High] — [ihchina.cn](https://www.ihchina.cn/luntan_details/27930.html); [Baidu (Russian)](https://baike.baidu.com/ru/item/%D0%9E%D0%B1%D1%80%D1%8F%D0%B4%20%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B6%D0%BA%D0%B8%20%D0%B2%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%BE%D1%81%20%D1%85%D0%B0%D0%B9%D1%81%D0%B8-%D0%BC%D0%BE%D0%BD%D0%B3%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%B2/857888).
- Subei (Gansu) Mongols: a 1984 article by 却拉布吉 describes the "Orbo" haircut as "Oirat Mongol folklore" (1982 fieldwork, a child named Chuluun/朝洛蒙). The blessing starts with "Ом!" and mentions golden and silver scissors, a white lion, a bodhi tree, and herds. [Medium; secondary citation] — cited in [ihchina.cn, 2023](https://www.ihchina.cn/luntan_details/27930.html).
- Oirat descendants in Qinghai mixed their customs with Tibetan, Han, Hui and Tu neighbours. [Medium] — [ihchina.cn](https://www.ihchina.cn/luntan_details/27930.html).

### Inferences
- The name "敖尔波" (Orbo) is probably a Chinese spelling of the Mongolian word **өрөвлөг** (first/baby hair). Mongolian dictionaries use өрөвлөг as another word for даахь ("даахь буюу өрөвлөг", [ugiinbichig.mn](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi)). This is my inference and is not stated by the source.
- The "western aimags" сонжуу/согсоо custom (section 2) is the only regional detail that may apply to Oirat groups in Mongolia. Its link to specific groups is unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- Torguud, Zakhchin, Ööld, Altai Uriankhai, Khoton: no source found. Khoton have a Turkic and Muslim-origin history, so their practice may differ, but I found no evidence either way.
- No source gives Oirat-specific ages, gifts, or hair disposal for groups inside Mongolia.

## 4. Buryat (Буриад): in Mongolia (Dornod, Khentii, Selenge, Bulgan) and in Buryatia (Russia); shamanic elements

### Takeaway
Sources disagree on who cuts first. Older Mongolian ethnography says the **woman who delivered the baby** (the midwife figure) cuts first. A 2025 report from **Tunka (Buryatia)** says the **maternal uncles (nagasa)** cut first. The Tunka version is held after age 1, once the soft spot (fontanelle) has closed, and the old hair-disposal custom is close to nature-spirit (animist) practice: hair was mixed with sheep's wool and left in a tree for birds. I found no current description for Buryats in Mongolia.

### Cited Findings
- Mongolian ethnographic text: "буриадад хүүхдийг эх барьж авсан буюу боож авсан эх нь эхэлж үсэнд хутга хайч хүргэн ёсолдог байна". Then the child is carried sunwise past the guests, each cuts a lock, and the mother wraps the hair in a khadag. [Medium] — [mn.wikipedia talk page](https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A5%D1%8D%D1%80%D1%8D%D0%B3%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3%D1%87%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD_%D1%8F%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B0:Pimp.at.mn); [PDFCOFFEE](https://pdfcoffee.com/--14601-pdf-free.html) (phrased as "хүйг боож авсан" (tied the cord) / "эх барьж авсан" (delivered)).
- English and Russian retellings translate this differently: "the birth mother or the God Mother that delivered the child" ([mongolmom 2016](https://mongolmom.blogspot.com/2016/04/mongolian-childrens-first-hair-cutting.html)); "the person who helped with childbirth had the privilege of cutting the hair first" ([ResearchGate paper, abstract/snippet only](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372946254_Keeping_the_Child_Hair_Cutting_Ceremony_in_the_Context_of_Mongolia_and_Inner_Mongolia)). [Medium]
- **Tunka (Buryatia), article of 4 April 2025:** [Medium] — [Burunen.ru](https://burunen.ru/news/society/136427-v-tunke-vozrozhdayut-iskonnyy-obychay-strizhki-volos/).
  - Names: "үһэ хайшалаан", also called **милаан** (linked by the article to the word "малаан").
  - Age: after the 1st birthday, only once the fontanelle has closed; otherwise it is postponed.
  - A **lama picks the day**. Example: a boy born 28 February had the rite on 30 March.
  - First cutters: the **nagasa** (mother's brothers); if none, the mother's elderly uncles.
  - Boys: the crown is cut first, and hair around the fontanelle is left. The article says girls are done differently but gives no detail.
  - Old form: parents carried the child on a tray clockwise past relatives, each cutting a lock. Hands and scissors were washed in clean water, mantras were said, and the scissors were cleaned with sheep's wool (for long life). The cut locks were **mixed with sheep's wool and put in a tree cleft so birds could use them for nests**, which counted as a good deed.
  - Gifts: livestock before, **money on a tray** now.
  - Modern form: guests just come to the centre of the hall and congratulate the family. Local people "are increasingly reviving" the Tunka version.
- Another Russian source says that for Buryat boys, reaching **age 5** was marked by a rite called "уhэнэй hара" ("month of hair"), with a formal haircut, after which the boy counted as a full member of the family. [Low] — search-result summary pointing to [legendtour.ru](https://legendtour.ru/mongolia/info/volosy.shtml) / [krasivyepricheski.com](https://krasivyepricheski.com/uroki/pricheska-u-buryat) (not opened; unclear which page says this).
- General Buryat family-custom pages for Pribaikalye (Irkutsk region) do not mention a first haircut at all. They describe a cradle rite at day 6–7 and naming by an older guest. [Medium, negative finding] — [etno.pribaikal.ru](https://etno.pribaikal.ru/%D0%B3%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%BD%D0%B0%D1%8F/%D0%B1%D1%83%D1%80%D1%8F%D1%82%D1%8B/%D1%81%D0%B5%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%B9%D0%BD%D0%BE-%D0%B1%D1%8B%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%8B%D0%B5-%D0%BE%D0%B1%D1%8B%D1%87%D0%B0%D0%B8-%D0%B1%D1%83%D1%80%D1%8F%D1%82/).
- Buryat settlement in Mongolia includes the Dashbalbar, Tsagaan-Ovoo, Bayan-Uul and Bayandun soums of Dornod (background only). [Low] — search result citing a [zindaa.mn interview](https://news.zindaa.mn/49zv).

### Inferences
- "эх барьж авсан ... эх" most likely means the **midwife** (in Mongolian, the woman who "catches" the baby is called "эх баригч" or "хүйн эх"), not the birth mother. The English "birth mother" reading seems to be a mistranslation. Report both readings.
- The Buryat versions (midwife first; maternal uncle first) both differ from Khalkh, where the father's side or the eldest guest leads.
- The Tunka "hair in a tree for birds" custom is the clearest shamanic or animist-style element found for any group. The source does not call it shamanic.

### Gaps
- No source found for how Buryats in Mongolia (Dornod, Khentii, Selenge, Bulgan) do the rite today.
- No source found for an explicitly shamanic (бөө) Buryat haircut rite. The Buryat term "үһэ абаха" was not found.
- Girls' version in Tunka not described.
- Rebecca Empson's *Harnessing Fortune* (2011), on Buryats in north-east Mongolia, may describe keeping hair in household chests, but I could not access the text to confirm.

## 5. Other groups: Дарьганга, Үзэмчин, Барга, Хамниган, Сартуул, Дархад, Хотгойд, Цаатан/Духа, Тува (Tuvan)

### Takeaway
Online I found **no** group-specific description for Dariganga, Üzemchin, Barga, Khamnigan, Sartuul, Darkhad, Tsaatan/Dukha, or the Tuvans of Mongolia. Khotgoid appear only in the shared Khalkh–Oirat list (same rule as Khalkh). The best Tuvan data comes from the **Tuvans of China (Xinjiang)**, documented in a 2017 doctoral dissertation.

### Cited Findings
- Khotgoid: the grandfather, father or oldest respected guest cuts first (same as Khalkh). [Medium] — [mn.wikipedia talk page](https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A5%D1%8D%D1%80%D1%8D%D0%B3%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3%D1%87%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD_%D1%8F%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B0:Pimp.at.mn). A Khotgoid heritage blog lists only "wedding rites, family customs…" with no haircut detail — [baaska2020 blog](http://baaska2020.blogspot.com/2013/01/blog-post_5999.html).
- **Tuvans of China** ("хылбык алыры", first haircut): [Medium] — [studfile.net preview of Yusha Zh. M., doctoral dissertation 2017](https://studfile.net/preview/16573349/page:15/).
  - Age about 1 to 5; time in the womb counts, so a 1-year-old counts as 2.
  - Before the rite the child wears a **tulum** hairstyle (womb hair left on forehead and temples).
  - Meaning: the cut ends infancy and the child's link to the other world; it strengthens the life force (*tyn*).
  - It is the largest childhood rite, and men can take part (unlike earlier childhood rites).
  - The **maternal uncle cuts first**, then elders go sunwise. If a family cannot afford a feast, the maternal uncle alone cuts, and that still counts as keeping the tradition.
  - Scissors are tied with a white khadag and **smoked with juniper**.
  - The child gets their own livestock for the first time; the **midwife** attends as an honoured guest and gets a gift.
  - Guests answer blessings with set phrases such as "Ындыг-ла болсун!" ("let it be so").
  - Hair is put in a bowl, sewn into a pouch the child wears, then kept in a chest with family heirlooms. It is called *erdine* ("treasure"). Losing it is **not** a bad sign.
  - A boiled sheep's head with heart pieces is offered at the household **Buddhist altar**.
  - Russian Tuvans (mostly Gelug Buddhists) reject such meat offerings and have made new blessings mentioning cars and money.
- Tsaatan/Dukha: only a TikTok video titled "Mongolian hair cutting ceremony in Taiga reindeer tribes" turned up, with no checkable content. [Low] — [TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@heykiera_/video/7421513866267446535).

### Inferences
- The Tuvans of China share the "maternal uncle first" and "midwife honoured" features with Kalmyk and Buryat sources. This suggests a wider northern and western pattern, separate from the Khalkh "eldest or grandfather" pattern.

### Gaps
- Dariganga, Üzemchin, Barga, Khamnigan, Sartuul, Darkhad, Tsaatan/Dukha, and the Tuvans of Tsengel (Bayan-Ölgii): nothing found online. Printed regional ethnographies, aimag museums, or fieldwork would be needed.

## 6. Kazakh (Казах) in Bayan-Ölgii

### Takeaway
Kazakhs have their **own, different** first-haircut custom. It is tied to **40 days after birth** (қырқынан шығару, "bringing the baby out of its forty days"), not to ages 2–5, and it has Islamic features (charity equal to the hair's weight). I found **no** source showing that Kazakhs in Bayan-Ölgii hold the Mongol-style сэвлэг/даахь үргээх ceremony, and no source that describes Bayan-Ölgii Kazakh practice specifically.

### Cited Findings
- Kazakh families cut a baby's hair at **40 days**: "казахи стригут малыша, когда ему исполняется 40 дней". In Orenburg (Russia) the **eldest man of the village** cut the first lock, saying "Жасың ұзақ болсын!" ("long life to you!"), then every man cut a lock; guests gave money or sweets. [Medium] — [rcrkoo.ru (Orenburg regional heritage register ORN065), 2014](https://rcrkoo.ru/archives/digital_catalogue/%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%85%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9-%D0%BE%D0%B1%D1%80%D1%8F%D0%B4-%D1%88%D0%B0%D1%88-%D0%B0%D0%BB%D1%83-%D0%BE%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%89%D0%B8%D0%B2%D0%B0%D0%BD).
- Boys may keep an **айдар** (crown lock), seen as protection against the evil eye. It is cut on the day of circumcision. [Medium] — [rcrkoo.ru](https://rcrkoo.ru/archives/digital_catalogue/%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%85%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B9-%D0%BE%D0%B1%D1%80%D1%8F%D0%B4-%D1%88%D0%B0%D1%88-%D0%B0%D0%BB%D1%83-%D0%BE%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%89%D0%B8%D0%B2%D0%B0%D0%BD).
- Boys are often brought out of the 40 days **before** day 40 (believed to mature faster); girls wait the **full 40 days**. The hair is kept, or buried where no one walks. In the south, the person who cuts the hair gets **gold equal to the hair's weight**; Islamic practice is to give silver or charity (садақа) equal to its weight. The baby is bathed in 40 spoons of water. [Medium] — [kznews.kz, 30 Dec 2018](https://kznews.kz/article/qyrqynan-shygaru-balanyn-shashyn-alu); bathing in 40 spoons also in [sunna.kz](https://sunna.kz/archives/22219) and [daylikz.com](https://daylikz.com/balany-%D2%9Byr%D2%9Bynan-%D2%9Balaj-shy%D2%93arady/) (search results).
- The Islamic view: қырқынан шығару is a local custom, not a religious rite, and is "permitted" (мубах). Islam recommends cutting on the **7th day**. [Medium] — [islam.kz](https://islam.kz/kk/articles/atamura/balany-qyrqynan-shygaru-dasturi-jane-onyn-dindegi-orny-2938/); [igz.kz](https://igz.kz/post/1075/); [ernur.kz](https://ernur.kz/dastur/narestenin-shashyn-40-kunde-ala-ma-7-kunde-me-kaisysy-durys) (search results).
- Islamic teaching quoted by kznews **forbids cutting only part of the hair and leaving the rest** ("шаштың бір бөлігін алып, бір бөлігін қалдыруға тыйым салды"). This is the opposite of the Mongol custom of leaving a tuft or locks. [Medium] — [kznews.kz](https://kznews.kz/article/qyrqynan-shygaru-balanyn-shashyn-alu).
- Some accounts say the cut hair is sewn onto the shoulder of the baby's clothes. [Low] — [orexca.com](https://www.orexca.com/kazakhstan/traditions/birth.htm) (search result).
- **Тұсау кесу** (cutting a cord tied around the child's legs at first steps) is a separate rite, with the eldest and most respected person invited. [Medium] — search results citing [orexca.com (Russian)](https://www.orexca.com/rus/kazakhstan/traditions/birth.htm).
- One report says some customs forgotten in Kazakhstan are "still firmly kept" in Bayan-Ölgii, but names none. [Low] — [qazaqstan.tv](https://qazaqstan.tv/news/74490/) (search result).

### Inferences
- Kazakhs in Bayan-Ölgii most likely follow the Kazakh 40-day pattern, not the Mongol 2–5-year ceremony. This is **not confirmed** by a Bayan-Ölgii source, so mark it as likely, not certain.
- Main differences from the Mongol version: much younger age (40 days vs 2–5 years); women's role in Kazakhstan vs elder men in Orenburg; Islamic charity equal to the hair's weight; no tuft left (Islamic rule), except the folk айдар for boys; no khadag or wooden knife.

### Gaps
- No Bayan-Ölgii-specific source on Kazakh haircut customs, and no source on whether mixed Kazakh–Mongol families there do both rites.

## 7. Inner Mongolia (China) and Kalmyk (Russia), briefly for comparison

### Takeaway
In Inner Mongolia, age varies by area (one month in Khorchin, 1 year in Ordos, odd ages 3/5/7 in Urad), and the first cutter is a **zodiac-compatible elder** or a lama. Kalmyks cut at **age 1**. The **maternal uncle** cuts first, scissors are bound with **red thread**, the hair is cut in four places and then fully shaved, and the uncle receives money.

### Cited Findings
- **Urad (Bayannuur, Inner Mongolia):** age depends on area, usually the odd ages 3, 5, 7. Both sexes keep two patches of hair: boys at the front and back fontanelles, girls on the two sides. A lama or zodiac-compatible elder is invited. The date is often the summer solstice or the birthday. [Low; search snippet only, page would not load] — [Inner Mongolia ICH platform](https://web.ichnmg.cn/project/detail.shtml?ichId=1026).
- **Khorchin** cut at one month (满月), **Ordos** at 1 year (周岁), and **Alasha** sets different ages by sex. [Low; snippet only, page blocked] — [Baidu 剪发礼](https://baike.baidu.com/item/%E5%89%AA%E5%8F%91%E7%A4%BC/7873020).
- In Inner Mongolia the first cutter is a "全人" ("complete person"): elderly, with a zodiac sign that does not clash with the child's, and with many children or brothers. [Low; snippet only] — [nmonline.com.cn](http://www.nmonline.com.cn/nmzx/culture_lbx2.asp?id=2639).
- **Kalmyk** (recorded interviews, Cambridge project): [Medium] — [Cambridge Kalmyk heritage](https://www.kalmykheritage.socanth.cam.ac.uk/common/kalmyksinrussia.php?classif=61&language=en).
  - First haircut at **age 1** by the child's **maternal uncle**; the scissor handles are bound with **red thread**.
  - Hair is cut from 4 places (front, back, both sides), then the head is fully shaved.
  - The hair is placed near images of deities, or sewn into clothing, or burned (one family).
  - The uncle gets a plate that relatives fill with money.
  - Lamas were consulted on good days for haircuts.
- Kalmyk: no cutting before age 1. Cut hair was sewn into a little bag on the child's clothes; if not lost, it was wrapped in white cloth and kept on the altar next to the бурхан (Buddha images). Hair was seen as the seat of life force, which evil spirits (шулмус) try to steal. Name given: "унһн үсн". [Medium] — [baylig.ru (Г. Корнеев), 15 Aug 2022](https://baylig.ru/nasledie/kak-obmanut-shulmusov/); hair kept in cloth sewn to clothing, then on altar, also in [kalmykia-online.ru](https://kalmykia-online.ru/tradition/1398) (search result).
- A 2010 dissertation says that among Kalmyks today "the archaic idea of the correct ritual form of cutting the first hair" still survives. [Medium] — [Sharaeva T. I., 2010, dissercat.com](https://www.dissercat.com/content/obryady-zhiznennogo-tsikla-u-kalmykov).
- Conflict: one academic paper says Kalmyk children's hair is cut at **age 5**, citing Empson (2011). [Low] — [ResearchGate (snippet)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372946254_Keeping_the_Child_Hair_Cutting_Ceremony_in_the_Context_of_Mongolia_and_Inner_Mongolia); contradicted by the [Cambridge interviews](https://www.kalmykheritage.socanth.cam.ac.uk/common/kalmyksinrussia.php?classif=61&language=en) and [baylig.ru](https://baylig.ru/nasledie/kak-obmanut-shulmusov/) (age 1).

### Inferences
- Kalmyk practice (age 1, maternal uncle, red thread, full shave) is closer to the Tunka Buryat and Tuvan patterns than to Khalkh.
- The "age 5 for Kalmyks" claim looks doubtful: Empson's fieldwork was with Buryats in Mongolia, not Kalmyks. Treat it as an error unless the paper can be read.

### Gaps
- Inner Mongolia pages (ichnmg.cn, baike.baidu.com, nmonline) could not be opened, so details rely on search snippets.
- Kalmyk boys-vs-girls differences in the first haircut are not described.

## 8. Nomadic/rural (in a ger) vs urban (Ulaanbaatar apartment, restaurant or hall) practice today

### Takeaway
In cities the ceremony is now often held in **restaurants or halls**, with catering, a hired blessing-speaker and music, and with a much bigger guest list (coworkers, friends). Critics say it has become "an excuse for an adults' party". The same move to halls is seen among Buryats (Tunka) and Haixi Mongols (ceremony held in the city of Xining). I found no source that describes current rural ger practice in detail.

### Cited Findings
- Traditionally a small feast for close relatives; now "хавтгайруулсан нийтийн найр" (a sprawling public feast) with "бүхий л хамаатан садан, найз нөхөд, ажлын газрынхныг" (all relatives, friends and coworkers). The author warns it should not be an excuse for an adults' party, and says traditional feasts had no alcohol. [Medium] — [arkhangai.mn, 8 Jun 2018](http://arkhangai.mn/index.php?newsid=3616) (also on [dayarmongol.mn](http://dayarmongol.mn/index.php?newsid=7086)).
- City families choose restaurants "that keep the national tradition" to save time; catering is very common; some restaurants offer a blessing-speaker (ерөөлч) and morin khuur (horse-head fiddle) music. [Low–Medium; advertising content] — [huree.mn venues list](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%82%D3%A9%D1%80%D1%81%D3%A9%D0%BD-%D3%A9%D0%B4%D3%A9%D1%80-%D2%AF%D1%81%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B9-%D0%BD%D0%B0%D0%B9%D1%80-%D1%85%D0%B8%D0%B9%D1%85%D1%8D/) (search result); [ikon.mn sponsored article, 16 Feb 2022](https://ikon.mn/n/2gs3) (a restaurant taking bookings for сэвлэг үргээх ёслол).
- No alcohol at the rite by tradition; mare's milk (айраг) may be sprinkled. [Low–Medium] — search results summarising [huree.mn](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D2%AF%D1%81%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B9-%D0%BD%D0%B0%D0%B9%D1%80%D0%BD%D1%8B-%D1%85%D0%BE%D0%BE%D0%BB/) and the Buryat/Mongol search set.
- Example of a city ceremony: an Ulaanbaatar apartment, guests coming and going, a festive table, the date set with Buddhist monks' help. [Low] — [mongol-note blog, 17 Feb 2011](http://mongol-note.blogspot.com/2011/02/blog-post_17.html).
- A city home ceremony at 8:00 am, with a cousin coming from Darkhan; boys and girls shaved alike at the end. [Low] — [Val Farmer, 4 Feb 2014](https://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html?m=1).
- Social exclusion today: divorced people are not invited, because their presence is believed to bring a broken marriage to the child. [Low; personal essay] — [Borderless Journal, Odbayar Dorj, 14 Apr 2025](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/).
- Buryat (Tunka) change: from carrying the child round relatives on a tray to guests coming to "the centre of the hall"; gifts changed from livestock to money. [Medium] — [Burunen.ru, 2025](https://burunen.ru/news/society/136427-v-tunke-vozrozhdayut-iskonnyy-obychay-strizhki-volos/).
- Haixi Mongols: relatives from Haixi and Haiyan travelled to the city of **Xining** for a 2021 ceremony, in traditional dress. [Medium] — [ihchina.cn, 2023](https://www.ihchina.cn/luntan_details/27930.html).
- Gifts have changed from sheep and lambs to toys, stationery and money. [High] — [arkhangai.mn 2018](http://arkhangai.mn/index.php?newsid=3616); [Zindaa 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); search summary of [mongolmedleg.org](https://mongolmedleg.org/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D0%B4%D0%B0%D0%B0%D1%85%D1%8C-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BE%D0%BD/).

### Inferences
- Urbanisation is changing the same things in Mongolia, Buryatia and Qinghai: venue (ger or home becomes hall or restaurant), gifts (livestock become money), and guest list (kin become a wide social network).

### Gaps
- No survey or data on how many families hold it in restaurants vs at home, or on differences in rural soums today.
- No source describes a present-day ger ceremony in the countryside in detail.

## 9. Buddhist-oriented, shamanist, and non-religious/modern families

### Takeaway
Buddhist elements are well documented across groups: **lamas choose the date** (Khalkh, Tunka Buryat, Haixi, Kalmyk), hair is kept near **Buddha images** (Kalmyk), there are offerings at the **altar** (Tuvans of China), and a child **meant to become a lama has all hair cut**. Clear evidence of a distinct *shamanist-family* version is **weak**. Scholars note that the blessing poems come from shamanic ritual songs, and the Tunka "hair in a tree for birds" custom looks animist. No source describes a separate non-religious version, only simplified city versions.

### Cited Findings
- "Лам болгох хүүхдийн даахийг бүгдийг авдаг ёстой" (a child to be made a lama has all of their hair cut). [Medium; standard dictionary] — [ugiinbichig.mn, citing Ч. Арьясүрэн & Х. Нямбуу, "Монгол ёс заншлын их тайлбар толь"](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi).
- Lamas choose the date: [Val Farmer 2014](https://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html?m=1) and [mongol-note 2011](http://mongol-note.blogspot.com/2011/02/blog-post_17.html) (Khalkh); [Burunen.ru 2025](https://burunen.ru/news/society/136427-v-tunke-vozrozhdayut-iskonnyy-obychay-strizhki-volos/) (Tunka Buryat, plus mantras); [ihchina.cn 2023](https://www.ihchina.cn/luntan_details/27930.html) (Haixi, lama divination); [Cambridge Kalmyk heritage](https://www.kalmykheritage.socanth.cam.ac.uk/common/kalmyksinrussia.php?classif=61&language=en) (Kalmyk, lamas consulted for good days). [High]
- Kalmyk hair kept "рядом с бурханами" (next to the Buddha images) on the altar. [Medium] — [baylig.ru 2022](https://baylig.ru/nasledie/kak-obmanut-shulmusov/).
- Tuvans of China put a sheep's head and heart pieces on the Buddhist altar; Russian Tuvans (Gelug) see such offerings as unacceptable in childhood rites. [Medium] — [Yusha 2017 dissertation (studfile)](https://studfile.net/preview/16573349/page:15/).
- The Haixi Mongol blessing poems (祝赞词) "evolved from shaman sacrificial words". Mongol rites mix shamanic traces with Tibetan Buddhism. [Medium; scholarly opinion] — [ihchina.cn 2023](https://www.ihchina.cn/luntan_details/27930.html).
- A 2020 press conference by a member of the Mongolian Shaman Association links the 9–20 month period and spirits (онгод, сахиус) to the child's soul and mentions that the даахь rite "used to be done". It gives no shaman-specific procedure. [Low] — [polit.mn, 21 Oct 2020](https://www.polit.mn/a/85212).
- Islamic families (Kazakh): the rite is classed as a permitted local custom; Islam prefers the 7th day and charity equal to the hair's weight. [Medium] — [islam.kz](https://islam.kz/kk/articles/atamura/balany-qyrqynan-shygaru-dasturi-jane-onyn-dindegi-orny-2938/); [kznews.kz 2018](https://kznews.kz/article/qyrqynan-shygaru-balanyn-shashyn-alu).

### Inferences
- In practice the "religious" difference shows up mainly in *who sets the date* (a lama vs a zurkhai/astrology calendar app or website vs no date check) and *where the hair goes* (altar or monastery vs family chest). It does not show up as separate rituals. This is inferred, not stated by any one source.

### Gaps
- No source describes a shamanist family's version step by step (for example, a бөө (shaman) doing the cut, or an offering to ongon spirits).
- No source describes how non-religious or modern families change the rite, beyond the restaurant trend.

## 10. Age and gender rules by group (boys vs girls)

### Takeaway
The common Mongolian rule is **boys at odd ages (3 or 5), girls at even ages (2 or 4)**, explained by arga (odd, male, action) and bilig (even, female, wisdom). But sources conflict: one gives girls 4 or 6, one blog reverses it, Urad uses odd ages 3/5/7, Haixi uses age 3 for all, Kalmyks and Tunka Buryats use age 1, and Kazakhs use 40 days.

### Cited Findings
- Boys 3 and 5, girls 2 and 4: "эрэгтэй хүүхдийг 3, 5 настайд нь эмэгтэй хүүхдийг 2, 4 настайд нь даахийг нь үргээнэ". ### Inferences
- The odd/even rule by sex seems to be a Khalkh-centred norm. Groups farther from central Mongolia (Kalmyk, Buryat, Tuvan, Haixi, Inner Mongolia) mostly use one fixed age for everyone (1 or 3) or local ages, and rarely split by sex.
- Since ages are counted in хий нас, a "3-year-old" boy may be about 2 by international counting. The final report should say this to avoid confusion.

### Gaps
- No source gives a sex-based age rule for Buryat, Kalmyk or Oirat groups inside Mongolia.
- No data on how strictly families follow the odd/even rule today (no surveys found).
