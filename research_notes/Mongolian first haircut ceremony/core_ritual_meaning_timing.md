## 1. Meaning, origin, and the literal meaning of the names

### Takeaway
"Сэвлэг" and "даахь" both mean a small child's first, never-cut hair. "Үргээх" is the polite verb used for removing it. The rite marks the child's move from babyhood into childhood. Guests bless the child with long life, good fortune and usefulness to parents and country. Cutting the first hair is believed to remove bad influences, bad temper and illness. Mongolian sources call the custom "very old" but give no dated historical evidence. The claims about shamanic roots and a Buddhist reshaping come from one weak English-language paper. The custom is on Mongolia's National Register of Intangible Cultural Heritage.

### Cited Findings
**Names and word meanings**
- The national dictionary entry for **СЭВЛЭГ** gives these senses:
  - (1) an honorific word for hair, and "Хүүхдийн даахь, гэзэг" (a child's hair or braid)
  - (2) "Нялх хүүхдийн онгон үс" (a baby's untouched, virgin hair)
  - (3) "Тав гэзэг" (a five-tuft hairstyle)
  - The entry also lists **"сэвлэг үргээх" = "хүүхдийн үс авах"** (to take a child's hair).
  - The dictionary quotes the word from *Монголын нууц товчоо* (the 13th-century Secret History): "Одоо бид хоёр сэвлэг үсээ хойш урагш унжуулж…". This shows the word itself is very old.
  - The dictionary has no separate entries for "даахь" or "өрөвлөг". — [mongoltoli.mn, СЭВЛЭГ](https://mongoltoli.mn/search.php?opt=1&ug_id=81271&word=%D0%A1%D0%AD%D0%92%D0%9B%D0%AD%D0%93) [single, but an authoritative dictionary]
- **"Даахь"** is defined as "Эхээс төрөөд хүүхдийн толгойд ургасан (нялх) үсийг даахь гэдэг" (the baby hair that grows on the head from birth). — [updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html); same idea in [zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn) [multi, but both are Family C]
- The other names for the rite are: **сэвлэг үргээх, даахь авах, даахь үргээх, өрөвлөг/өрвөг авах, үсний найр**. One search summary noted "the name changes but the custom is basically the same" (snippet only). — [24tsag.mn, 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755); [ugiinbichig.mn, citing Арьяасүрэн & Нямбуу](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi); search summary of [gogo.mn](https://gogo.mn/r/jwol1) [multi]
- Professor С.Дулам lists these names: "даахь авах, даахь үргээх, даахь тайрах, үс авах" (snippet only; the page would not load). — [tusgal.mn, С.Дулам](http://tusgal.mn/s-dulam-khuukhdiin-daakhiig-purew-garagt-awdaggui-baisan) [single]
- mongolcom.mn reportedly says the hair of a 1-year-old is called "сэвлэг" and the hair of a 3–5-year-old is called "даахь" (snippet only; the site was down). — [mongolcom.mn](http://mongolcom.mn/read/8394) [single, unverified; the dictionary does not support this split]
- The official English name used by the National Center for Cultural Heritage (Соёлын өвийн үндэсний төв) is "Customs related to the cutting child's hair for the first time" (Хүүхдийн даахь үргээх ёслол). It is registered in the "traditional festivals, rituals, customs…" domain of the **National Register of Intangible Cultural Heritage of Mongolia**. — [IRCI database record, NCCH 2022 audio-visual material](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13419) [single, official]

**Meaning and beliefs**
- Belief: if the baby hair is not taken, the child "will be angry and not clever" (ууртай, ухаан муутай болно). — [updown.mn](https://updown.mn/80564.html); [zindaa.mn](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); [huree.mn, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/) [multi, but all Family C]
- A 2024 qualitative study observed eight ceremonies, seven in Mongolia, and interviewed nine people. Its findings:
  - The purpose is to bless the child, "block misfortunes", keep the first hair, and celebrate with family.
  - If a child has a bad temper, an illness or a delay in development, the haircut is expected to heal it, because "bad things will be gone through the first haircut".
  - — [Javzandulam, Lkhagvadorj & WuDong, *Nomadic Studies* 31, 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25) [single, academic]
- Blessing themes: long life, lasting happiness, being kind to father and helpful to mother, serving the country, and being respected or a leader among people. — [ikon.mn, 2022](https://ikon.mn/n/2gs3); [24tsag.mn, 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755); [updown.mn](https://updown.mn/80564.html); [IRCI/NCCH](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13419) [multi]
- The tufts left on the head carry meaning: the boy's tuft (тэвэг) stands for becoming a "төрийн хар хүн" (a lay man who serves the state), and the girl's two tufts (хөхөл) stand for becoming a "гэрийн эх хүн" (the mother of a household). — [24tsag.mn, 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553); [medeelelzui blog, citing Сампилдэндэв et al.](https://medeelelzui.blogmn.net/44325/huuhdiin-daahi-urgeeh-yos.html) [multi, but both Family A]
- The rite is held once the child can understand that the party is for them ("өөртөө зориулан баяр хийж буйг мэдрэх"). This is why it is a bigger event than the newborn washing (угаалга). — [ugiinbichig.mn (dictionary)](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi); [namurana blog, citing Чингэл](http://namurana.blogspot.com/2011/08/blog-post_6221.html); [medeelelzui](https://medeelelzui.blogmn.net/44325/huuhdiin-daahi-urgeeh-yos.html) [multi]
- С.Дулам reportedly counts it as one of the three big rites of a person's life: угаалга, даахь үргээх and хурим (snippet only, from a search summary; not verified). — [tusgal.mn](http://tusgal.mn/s-dulam-khuukhdiin-daakhiig-purew-garagt-awdaggui-baisan) [single, unverified]

**History and origins**
- **Shamanic roots, Mongol Empire and Buddhism:** A 2025 paper says:
  - the rite "dates back to ancient nomadic traditions and has its roots in shamanistic practices"
  - in the Mongol Empire it became a public event that Genghis Khan used "to promote unity"
  - after Buddhism came in the 16th century, monks added scriptures, amulets and sacred threads
  - — [Chuluun, Batsaikhan & Lkhagvadorj, *J. of Education Culture and Society* 2025(2)](https://www.jecs.pl/index.php/jecs/article/view/1790) [single, LOW reliability: see Inferences]
- The same 2025 paper says the ceremony "begins with a Buddhist monk reciting sacred scriptures" (it cites Boykova 2021). — [JECS 2025](https://www.jecs.pl/index.php/jecs/article/view/1790) [single]
  - No Mongolian-language source I read puts a lama at the haircut itself.
- The 2024 study lists the ritual's links to: "horoscope, nomadic ethics, verbal poetry, belief and remedy, traditional medicine, Buddhism…". It says the timing and the first cutter "are determined by the Buddhist calendar" (citing Osor & Chimed 2013). — [Nomadic Studies 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25) [single]
- A US visitor in 2014 wrote: "Buddhist lamas traditionally advised families on the best days and years." — [drvalfarmer blog, 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html) [single, foreign observer]
- Mongolian news sites only say the custom is from "эрт дээр үеэс" (ancient times). They give no dated record. — [24tsag.mn 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553); [24tsag.mn 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755); [ikon.mn 2022](https://ikon.mn/n/2gs3) [multi]
- When a child is to become a lama, ALL of the hair is taken (it is not left in tufts). — [ugiinbichig.mn, citing Арьяасүрэн & Нямбуу](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi) [single]
- Older ethnography exists but I could not open it. The JECS 2025 reference list includes:
  - Vyatkina (1960), from the USSR–MPR expedition of 1948–49
  - Sampildendev (1985), *Malchin ardiin zan üiliin ulamjlal*
  - Otgon (2014), a PhD thesis on life-cycle symbols
  - — [JECS 2025](https://www.jecs.pl/index.php/jecs/article/view/1790)

### Inferences
- **The JECS 2025 history claims should NOT be used as fact.**
  - The Genghis Khan and Mongol Empire claim is cited to Munkhnasan (2021), a paper about Mongolian proper names. It is very unlikely to support a claim about state ceremonies.
  - The same section has clearly garbled text. It speaks of "the ear, after being cut during the ceremony", which the family "may consume". This is almost certainly a mistranslation of "hair".
  - Use only cautious wording, such as "some scholars link it to pre-Buddhist beliefs".
- The safe, well-supported statements are:
  - (a) the word "сэвлэг" for hair already appears in the 13th-century Secret History
  - (b) the rite is very old in folk memory
  - (c) it is mixed with Buddhist astrology (зурхай: choosing days by the lunar calendar and the 12 animal years) and with lamas advising on dates
  - (d) the belief that hair holds the child's health, used as a folk cure later, fits wider Inner Asian folk belief
- Literal meaning of **"үргээх"** (unsourced, from general Mongolian vocabulary; please verify): it is the causative form of "үргэх" (to take fright, to shy, as a horse does), so literally "to startle away". The rite uses it as a polite, respectful word instead of the plain "тайрах/хайчлах" (to cut).
- "Үсний найр" literally means "hair feast" (үс = hair, найр = feast or party). It is the everyday name, especially for today's restaurant parties.

### Gaps
- I found no Mongolian academic source with direct historical evidence, such as a Yuan, Qing or 19th-century record of the child's first-haircut rite, or a clear pre-Buddhist or shamanic origin. The relevant books are not online:
  - Сампилдэндэв 1985 and 2006
  - Бадамхатан's *Халхын угсаатны зүй*
  - Арьяасүрэн & Нямбуу's dictionary
- I could not confirm a Buddhist text recited specifically for this rite.
- The С.Дулам interview (tusgal.mn) would not load. Only its title and snippets were seen.

## 2. Age: boys vs girls, and how age is counted

### Takeaway
Nearly all sources agree on the rule: **boys at an odd age (usually 3 or 5), girls at an even age (usually 2 or 4).** The reason is number symbolism: odd = арга (male, "method"), even = билиг (female, "wisdom"). The age is the traditional Mongolian age (хий нас), not the Western birthday age. In the traditional count a baby is 1 at birth and gains a year at each Tsagaan Sar (Lunar New Year). Some variants exist: 6 or 7, girls historically only lightly cut at 7 or 9, and boys at 1 in one academic source. One foreign blog has the rule backwards.

### Cited Findings
- **Boys 3 or 5, girls 2 or 4:**
  - [IRCI/NCCH official record](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13419)
  - [ugiinbichig.mn (Арьяасүрэн & Нямбуу dictionary)](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi)
  - [namurana blog (Чингэл dictionary)](http://namurana.blogspot.com/2011/08/blog-post_6221.html)
  - [medeelelzui (Sampildendev)](https://medeelelzui.blogmn.net/44325/huuhdiin-daahi-urgeeh-yos.html)
  - [zindaa.mn](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)
  - [mongolmom blog 2016](https://mongolmom.blogspot.com/2016/04/mongolian-childrens-first-hair-cutting.html)
  - [borderlessjournal 2025](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/)
  - [multi, many independent families]
- **The reason, odd = арга (male) and even = билиг (female):** [ikon.mn 2022](https://ikon.mn/n/2gs3); [24tsag.mn 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755); [borderlessjournal 2025](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/); [medeelelzui](https://medeelelzui.blogmn.net/44325/huuhdiin-daahi-urgeeh-yos.html) [multi]
- **Oral-tradition variant:** "Охин хүүхдийн даахийг хөхөөн дуунаар 2, 4, 6 насанд, бүстэй хүүхдийн даахийг бугын дуунаар 3, 5, 7 насанд нь үргээ" (a girl's at 2, 4 or 6 "with the cuckoo's call"; a boy's, literally "a child with a belt", at 3, 5 or 7 "with the stag's call"). — [updown.mn 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html) [single text line]
- **Historically girls' hair** was sometimes only lightly touched at 7 or 9. — [zindaa.mn](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); [huree.mn](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/) [Family C only]
- **Academic variant:** "at the age of 1, 3, or 5 for boys or at the age of 2 or 4 for girls" (citing Gelegjamts 2015). The same study notes:
  - Dörvöd families cut at 3 or 5 whatever the child's sex
  - Kalmyks cut at 5
  - in Inner Mongolia it is 12
  - — [Nomadic Studies 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25) [single]
- **Family habit:** many families use the same age for all later children as they used for the first-born. — [zindaa.mn](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); [huree.mn](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/) [Family C]
- **Alternatively,** some families cut all the children's hair at once when the first-born's is cut (citing Sampildendev et al. 2006). — [Nomadic Studies 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25) [single]
- **Age counting:** In 2016, the Gandan lama-astrologer Д.Цогтбаатар gave these examples:
  - a girl born in 2015 (Sheep year) is "2 years old"
  - a boy born in 2014 (Horse year) is "3"
  - a girl born in 2013 (Snake year) is "4"
  - a boy born in 2012 (Dragon year) is "5"
  - The fetched summary says he gives these ages by "хий нас". — [ikon.mn, Д.Цогтбаатар, 8 Feb 2016](https://ikon.mn/n/o1f) [single, expert]
- **General range:** "Typically performed on children between the ages of 2 and 5." — [JECS 2025](https://www.jecs.pl/index.php/jecs/article/view/1790); [drvalfarmer 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html) [multi]
- **Contradiction:** the drvalfarmer blog says "boys in even years, girls in odd years". This is the opposite of every Mongolian source. — [drvalfarmer 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html) [single; treat as an error]

### Inferences
- Цогтбаатар's examples only make sense in the traditional count. A child born in 2015 cannot be 2 by Western count in early 2016. So **the age in the rule is the Mongolian traditional age (хий нас):**
  - a child is 1 at birth
  - the child gains a year at each Tsagaan Sar
  - Example: a boy born in spring 2023 is "3" from Tsagaan Sar 2025, so he could have the rite in 2025, when he is about 2 by Western count.
  - Hosts should check with an astrologer or lama, because some families now use the Western (birthday) age. I found no source that discusses this mismatch directly.
- **Most common today:** boys at 3 (sometimes 5), girls at 2 (sometimes 4). Odd and even always follow the boy/girl rule.

### Gaps
- No source states clearly whether today's urban families mostly use хий нас or Western age.
- I found no survey data on the most common age in 2020–2026.

## 3. How the day is chosen (lunar calendar, зурхай, animal years, seasons, weekdays)

### Takeaway
The family picks a "good month and good day" (сарын сайн, өдрийн өлзий). They usually ask a lama or astrologer, or use the lunar calendar (зурхай: Mongolian astrology based on the lunar calendar and the 12 animal years). Common rules:
- the waxing half of the lunar month (days 1–15)
- the child's "ивээл" months (the months of the child's friendly animal group)
- the Lunar New Year month (сар шинэ)
- traditionally, mid-summer for girls ("when the cuckoo calls") and mid-autumn for boys ("when the stag calls")
- Monday or Friday preferred; **Thursday avoided** (it is the "хүний хишиг өдөр")
- in the morning, before noon
- avoid the cold seasons

Today many city families simply choose a weekend.

### Cited Findings
**Lunar month and season**
- Д.Цогтбаатар (Gandan lama; editor of the astrology paper "Дорно өрнийн сарын зурхай"): "Хүүхдийн даахийг сар шинээр эсвэл ивээл сард нь авдаг" (done in the Lunar New Year month or in the child's ivee month). He adds:
  - Mongolians try to fit such events into the 1st–15th day of the lunar month ("шинийн 15-нд багтааж"), because the moon grows from the 1st to the 15th
  - for each child he listed the ivee months, favorable lunar days (for example, the 3rd or 4th of the new month) and a good hour (for example, 11:40–13:40)
  - — [ikon.mn, 2016](https://ikon.mn/n/o1f) [single, expert]
- "Гол төлөв зуны дунд сарын шинийн найманд охины, намрын дунд сарын шинийн 15-нд /Даваа, Баасан гараг таарвал бүр сайн/ хүүгийн даахийг үргээнэ." (Usually a girl's on the 8th of the mid-summer month, a boy's on the 15th of the mid-autumn month; even better if it falls on a Monday or Friday.) — [updown.mn 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html) [single text line]
- The 24tsag.mn 2017 article (Family A) gives preferred months as mid-autumn or mid-summer, days as the 8th or 15th, and weekdays as Monday or Friday. — [24tsag.mn 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553) [supports the above; a different family, so multi]
- Cuckoo and deer timing: a girl's hair in mid-summer "guided by the call of the cuckoo"; a boy's in mid-autumn "following the sound of the stag" (буга урамдах цаг). — [borderlessjournal 2025](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/); [updown.mn](https://updown.mn/80564.html); search summary of [gogo.mn](https://gogo.mn/r/jwol1) [multi]
- IRCI/NCCH: boys "in autumn", girls "in summer". — [IRCI](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13419) [official]
- **Seasons to avoid:** "Until the end of the spring season (Khansh neekh) and after the fall, it is not preferred… because the child might feel cold and have a head sickness." — [Nomadic Studies 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25) [single]

**Favorable lunar days (шинийн …)**

  - 5: livestock increase
  - 6: good complexion
  - 8: long life
  - 10: vigour
  - 11: sharp mind
  - 13: joy
  - 14: livestock increase
  - 15: "өлзийтэй сайн" (good fortune)
  - 19: good companions
  - 22: plenty of food and drink
  - 23: goods
  - 26: lasting joy
  - 27: good fortune
  - Both pages also say: check the weekday ("гаригийн сайн") too, not only the lunar day.
  - — [ikon.mn 2022 (an advertorial)](https://ikon.mn/n/2gs3); [huree.mn 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/) [two sites, likely one shared text]
- One page says a day is auspicious when "Балжинням" and "Дашням" fall together, according to "өрнийн зурхай". — [24tsag.mn 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755) [single]

**Weekdays**
- **Thursday (Пүрэв) is avoided** because it is "хүний хишиг өдөр" (a day of people's blessing or fortune). — [24tsag.mn 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553); [24tsag.mn 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755); headline of the С.Дулам interview, "Хүүхдийн даахийг пүрэв гарагт авдаггүй байсан" (the child's hair was not taken on Thursday), at [tusgal.mn](http://tusgal.mn/s-dulam-khuukhdiin-daakhiig-purew-garagt-awdaggui-baisan) [multi; Dulam is a leading folklorist]
- Family C writes: "Зарим нутагт дөрөв дэх өдөр хүүхдийн даахийг авдаггүй… энэ өдрийг хүний хишиг өдөр гэж үздэг" (in some places the hair is not taken on "the fourth day"). — [updown.mn](https://updown.mn/80564.html); [zindaa.mn](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); [huree.mn](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/)
- **Monday and Friday are best.** — [updown.mn](https://updown.mn/80564.html); [24tsag.mn 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553); [24tsag.mn 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755) [multi]

**Time of day**
- In the morning or before noon (бага үдээс өмнө; "наран залуу үед"). — [ugiinbichig.mn](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi); [namurana](http://namurana.blogspot.com/2011/08/blog-post_6221.html); [updown.mn](https://updown.mn/80564.html); [zindaa.mn](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn) [multi]

**Who decides, and modern practice**
- "Buddhist lamas traditionally advised families on the best days and years." — [drvalfarmer 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html). The 2024 study says timing is set by "the Buddhist calendar". — [Nomadic Studies 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25) [multi]
- **Modern change:** "Many families now hold the ceremony on weekends or at times that suit their personal schedules", because the astrological days clash with city work life. — [JECS 2025, citing Munkhnasan 2021](https://www.jecs.pl/index.php/jecs/article/view/1790). The 2024 study lists "busy life and scheduling conflicts" as a modern challenge. — [Nomadic Studies 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25) [multi]
- One family in 2014 held it during the Tsagaan Sar holidays. — [drvalfarmer 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html) [single]

**Animal years ("ивээл жил")**
- "Ивээл жил" means the friendly years in the 12-year cycle: the years that fall 5 and 9 years from your birth year. There are four groups of three:
  - Tiger–Dog–Horse (Бар–Нохой–Морь)
  - Mouse–Dragon–Monkey (Хулгана–Луу–Бич)
  - Ox–Snake–Rooster (Үхэр–Могой–Тахиа)
  - Pig–Sheep–Rabbit (Гахай–Хонь–Туулай)
  - A friendly year with a clashing element (махбод) can still cause friction.
  - (From a search summary; page not opened.) — [ugluu.mn](https://ugluu.mn/219916.html); dictionary entry [mongoltoli.mn "ивээл жил"](https://mongoltoli.mn/dictionary/detail/51241) [snippet only]

### Inferences
- Цогтбаатар's "ивээл сар" lists match the same three-animal groups, mapped onto the Mongolian lunar months: the 1st spring month is the Tiger month, the 2nd spring month the Rabbit month, and so on. Examples:
  - Horse-year boy: 1st spring month (Tiger), mid-summer (Horse), last autumn month (Dog)
  - Sheep-year girl: mid-spring (Rabbit), last summer month (Sheep), 1st winter month (Pig)
  - Snake-year girl: 1st summer month (Snake), mid-autumn (Rooster), last winter month (Ox)
  - **So the child's "ivee months" are the lunar months that carry the animals of the child's friendly group.** (My own reasoning from the month–animal order; please verify with an astrologer.)
- Note that Family C's "4th day" is ambiguous. In Mongolian, "дөрөв дэх өдөр" commonly means Thursday (Пүрэв, the 4th weekday). The 4th lunar day appears on the "good days" list, and both 24tsag articles and С.Дулам name Thursday. So **"the 4th day" almost certainly means Thursday.**
- **Most common today:** parents ask a lama or astrologer at a monastery (or use a printed lunar calendar) for a good day in the child's ivee month and waxing moon, then often move it to the nearest Saturday or Sunday. They choose a restaurant booking around midday. Thursday is still widely avoided.

### Gaps
- I found no source naming specific "bad months" (such as an "ominous month") for this rite. I also found no source on "харш" (clashing) years and this rite. Цогтбаатар's "харш" rules in the same article were for weddings only.
- I did not find a Gandan or other monastery page that offers a "даахь үргээх" date service with a procedure.
- The meaning of "Балжинням / Дашням" in this context was not explained by any source.

## 4. Step-by-step procedure (scissors, khadag, milk, who cuts first, words, gifts, final shave, tufts)

### Takeaway
The core sequence is the same in all source families:
1. The feast leader announces: "Now let us take our son's/daughter's даахь."
2. The child stands.
3. The first cutter (a person of the child's friendly animal year, or the most respected elder) first touches the hair with a **wooden knife**.
4. The same person then snips a lock with **scissors tied with a khadag** (a ceremonial silk scarf). They start at the **right temple for a boy, left for a girl**, and tie the lock into the khadag.
5. The child is given **milk** to taste, and milk is dabbed on the forehead. A blessing is said.
6. The child goes **sunwise (clockwise)** around all the guests. Each guest snips a small lock, says a blessing and gives a gift.
7. The mother collects the hair in the khadag.
8. Later the head is shaved or trimmed. Traditionally tufts were left: a forelock (тэвэг) for boys and two side tufts (хөхөл/санчиг) for girls. A lock is kept for anyone important who is absent.

### Inferences
- **Most common version today** (pulling the sources together):
  1. The MC or feast leader announces the rite.
  2. The child (in a deel) stands at the head of the table.
  3. The first cutter (ideally a person of the child's ivee year, otherwise the most respected elder or a grandparent) touches the hair with a wooden knife (often skipped in cities). They cut a lock with the khadag-tied scissors (right temple for a boy, left for a girl) and tie it into the khadag.
  4. The child tastes milk and gets a dab of milk on the forehead, and the first cutter says a blessing and gives a gift.
  5. All guests then cut in turn, sunwise, each with a short blessing and a gift (usually money in an envelope, or a toy).
  6. The mother keeps the khadag with the hair.
  7. A full shave or trim follows later, usually at home or at a barber, not in front of guests.
  8. Tufts are rarely left today, except a lock for an absent relative.
  - The "envelope" and "barber" details are my reasoning from the modern-practice sources; no source states them directly.
- The **wooden knife before steel scissors** (and the drop of milk on the blade) makes the first touch gentle and "pure", and keeps bare metal from being the first thing to "cut" the child. This is consistent with the "домноох" (charm) wording, but it is my interpretation.
- **"Хохол" and "шүүрс" do not appear** in any Mongolian source I read. "Хохол" is a Russian word for a forelock. The correct Mongolian terms are: **тэвэг / согсоо / ганц тав** (boy's forelock), **хөхөл / санчиг / сонжуу** (girl's side tufts), and **эвэр** (general word for the uncut patches).

### Gaps
- No source says clearly **who does the final full shave today** (parent, barber or elder), or whether it happens at the party.
- There is a conflict about where the boy's тэвэг sits. IRCI and Nomadic Studies say above the forehead. The medeelelzui page summary suggested the back of the head, but this may be a reading error.
- No source gives the exact length or size of the lock each guest should cut, or how many guests should cut.
- I found no source on a specific "хайч барих хүн" (scissors-holder) role separate from the first cutter.

## 5. What is done with the cut hair

### Takeaway
The hair is **kept, never thrown away**. The mother wraps it in the khadag (often a blue one) and stores it. If the child later falls ill, a little can be used as a folk cure: it is "smoked", or burned as a fumigation (smoke used for cleansing). Throwing it away carelessly is a strong taboo, because birds (such as magpies) may build nests with it and bring misfortune. I found **no Mongolian source** that tells families to bury or burn the whole bundle.

### Cited Findings
- The mother collects the cut hair and wraps it in a khadag ("эх нь сэвийг нь авч хадганд боодог"). — [24tsag.mn 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553); [24tsag.mn 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755); [medeelelzui](https://medeelelzui.blogmn.net/44325/huuhdiin-daahi-urgeeh-yos.html) [Family A]
- The hair is kept in the khadag. — [zindaa.mn](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); [huree.mn](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/); [mongolmom](https://mongolmom.blogspot.com/2016/04/mongolian-childrens-first-hair-cutting.html); [borderlessjournal](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/) [multi]

Snippet only, from a search summary of the 24tsag and gogo pages. — [24tsag.mn](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553) / [gogo.mn](https://gogo.mn/r/jwol1). Zindaa says the khadag-tied hair "was believed to help with healing". — [zindaa.mn](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn) [multi]
- "The first hair is not thrown away because it is considered sacred. When the child gets sick, even the adult family members smoke out of the first hair to expel the evil and sickness." — [Nomadic Studies 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25) [single, academic; supports the remedy use]
- A small piece of the first hair is "kept together with a coin (zoos) and milk butter (öröm) until the child reaches twelve years of age and is considered an adult". The context in the paper mixes Mongolia and Inner Mongolia and links this to the maternal-side elder (нагац). — [Nomadic Studies 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25) [single; region unclear]
- The family keeps the khadag and gives it to the child in the late pre-teen or early teen years. — [drvalfarmer 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html) [single]
- **Taboo:** "Үс гэзэгний сэвээ хамаагүй хаяхыг онцгой цээрлэдэг. 'Буянд цөв суулгаж, муу ёр дуудна'… Хээр гадаа хаясан сэвээр шаазгай мэт үүр барьдаг шувуу үүр барихдаа хэрэглэх учир зовлонд унагана" (throwing hair away carelessly is strictly avoided: it damages merit and calls bad omens; birds like magpies use hair thrown outside for nests, which brings suffering). — [updown.mn](https://updown.mn/80564.html) [single text line, Family C]
- JECS 2025 says the cut hair "is either kept as a sacred keepsake or offered for sacrifice, depending on local customs", and that it "may be consumed by the family" to cure illness. — [JECS 2025](https://www.jecs.pl/index.php/jecs/article/view/1790) [single; the text is garbled ("ear" for hair); LOW reliability]

### Inferences
- **The correct, most common practice:** keep the hair tied in the khadag, stored safely at home (in the family chest or a clean high place). Do not throw it away, do not leave it outside, and do not burn the whole bundle. Burning a few hairs as smoke is a special healing use, not a way of getting rid of it.
- Some families may later hand the bundle to the child when grown (teen years, or age 12 in one account). This is optional.

### Gaps
- No source describes a special "үсний уут" (hair pouch). The sources say "хадаг" only.
- No source says what happens to the hair if the child dies, or after the child is grown. No source gives burial customs.
- No source says where exactly in the home to store it (for example "in the chest" or "beside the Buddha shrine"). This is unsourced.

## 6. Roles: parents, grandparents, first cutter, feast leader/MC, lama

### Takeaway
- **Feast leader (найрын ахлагч):** often the father, grandfather or host; today often a hired MC (хөтлөгч) at a restaurant. Opens the rite.
- **First cutter:** in Mongolia, ideally a person born in one of the **child's ivee (friendly) years**. Цогтбаатар says a man. Other sources say the **most respected elder** (grandfather, father) among the Khalkha, Dörvöd, Bayad, Myangad and Khotgoid. Among the **Buryats**, the woman who helped deliver the baby cuts first.
- **Mother:** gathers the hair.
- **Father's-side and mother's-side uncles and aunts (авга, нагац):** should be present.
- **Lama:** mainly chooses the day. Only one academic source has a monk reciting at the event.

### Cited Findings
- **Ivee-year person first:**
  - "Хүүхдийн даахинд хамгийн эхэнд ивээл жилтэй эрэгтэй хүн хүрэх" (a MAN of the ivee year touches the hair first). — [Д.Цогтбаатар, ikon.mn 2016](https://ikon.mn/n/o1f)
  - "даахь үргээлэгчийн ивээл жилтнийг урьдаг ёстой"; the ivee-year person touches the hair first with the wooden knife. — [updown.mn](https://updown.mn/80564.html)
  - The ivee-year person sits at the right-rear and is the most honoured guest; in some places they touch the hair first. — [zindaa.mn](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)
  - "In Mongolia, the person with a pleasant year (iveel jil)… [is] honoured for the responsibility." — [Nomadic Studies 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25)
  - IRCI: someone "born in the same zodiac year" sits behind the child. — [IRCI](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13419)
  - [multi, strong]

- In practice it can be a young person: in 2014 "a young cousin whose lunar birth year matched the child's cut first". A knowledgeable older neighbour guided the family and cut second. — [drvalfarmer](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html) [single]
- **Father, grandfather or feast leader:** announces the start ("ёслолыг эхлүүлнэ"). — [zindaa.mn](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); [huree.mn](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/); [24tsag.mn 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755) [multi]
- **Mother:** collects the hair into the khadag. — [24tsag.mn 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553); [medeelelzui](https://medeelelzui.blogmn.net/44325/huuhdiin-daahi-urgeeh-yos.html) [Family A]
- **Uncles and aunts:** "Энэ ёслолд хүүхдийн авга, нагац нар заавал байх учиртай" (the child's paternal and maternal relatives must be present). — [updown.mn](https://updown.mn/80564.html) [single]. The oldest of the maternal family (нагац) "plays a significant role". — [Nomadic Studies 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25) [single]
- **Restaurant MC:** today most families hold it in restaurants, and the host (хөтлөгч) "drives" the event. — [huree.mn 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/); restaurants advertise packages for it — [ikon.mn 2022 advertorial](https://ikon.mn/n/2gs3) [multi]
- **Lama:** advises on the date — [drvalfarmer](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html); [ikon.mn (lama-astrologer)](https://ikon.mn/n/o1f). A monk recites scripture at the start — [JECS 2025](https://www.jecs.pl/index.php/jecs/article/view/1790) [single]

### Inferences
- **Best practice for a host who wants the "most correct" version:**
  1. Ask the astrologer which years are the child's ivee years.
  2. Invite a respected, happily married, successful relative born in one of those years to cut first. Цогтбаатар's version says it should be a man.
  3. If no such person is available, use the eldest respected relative (a grandparent).
- The ivee-year rule (from зурхай) and the eldest-person rule (older ethnographic sources) are two layers of the tradition. Today's urban practice mostly follows the ivee-year rule.

### Gaps
- No source explains whether the first cutter should be male only. Only Цогтбаатар says so. Other sources say "person".
- No source gives a fixed order for the guests after the first cutter, beyond "sunwise" or "by age".

## 7. Taboos and things to avoid

### Takeaway
Well-supported taboos:
- doing it on **Thursday**
- **throwing the hair away** carelessly
- leaving the child's hair **loose or tangled** afterwards (a sign of mourning, believed to shorten life)
- **cutting with unpurified scissors**: purify with juniper or incense first; touch with the wooden knife first
- holding it in **cold seasons**

Weaker, single-source taboos:
- a **divorced person** should not touch the hair
- **heavy drinking** at the party is criticised

I found **no sources** for: unlucky numbers, a crying child, sharp gifts, guests of clashing zodiac signs, or cutting direction rules beyond "right for boys, left for girls" and "sunwise".

### Cited Findings
- **Thursday:** avoided. — [24tsag.mn 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553); [24tsag.mn 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755); [С.Дулам headline](http://tusgal.mn/s-dulam-khuukhdiin-daakhiig-purew-garagt-awdaggui-baisan); ["4th day", Family C](https://updown.mn/80564.html) [multi]
- **Do not throw the hair away** (magpie nests, damage to merit). — [updown.mn](https://updown.mn/80564.html); [Nomadic Studies 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25) ("not thrown away… sacred") [multi]
- **Loose or tangled hair:** "үс гэзгийг задгай тавьж ширэлдүүлэхийг нас богиносно, буян хишиг барагдана гэж цээрлэдэг" (loose, matted hair is believed to shorten life and use up good fortune). Only grieving people let their hair loose. So after the rite the hair is braided and bound. — [updown.mn](https://updown.mn/80564.html); [zindaa.mn](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); [borderlessjournal](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/) [multi]
- **Purify the scissors** with арц or хүж (juniper or incense) before touching the hair. — [zindaa.mn](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); [huree.mn](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/) [Family C]
- **Milk drop on the blade** "so that the blade does not harm the child". — [borderlessjournal](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/) [single]
- **Cold seasons:** avoid before the end of spring and after autumn (risk of a cold or "head sickness"). — [Nomadic Studies 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25) [single]
- **Divorced people** are not invited to touch the hair, because "the child may later face a broken marriage". The author was a divorced Mongolian mother writing in 2025. — [borderlessjournal 2025](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/) [single, personal account]
- **Alcohol:** "extended and alcohol-infused celebrations" are named as a departure from tradition. — [Nomadic Studies 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25). A Mongolian opinion piece is titled "Хүүхдийн үсний найр томчуудын 'архидах'-ын шалтаг биш" (a child's hair party is not an excuse for adults to drink). — [updown.mn/50592](https://updown.mn/50592.html) (title only; not opened) [multi]
- **Modern songs and blessings** replacing traditional ones are listed as a "dilution". — [Nomadic Studies 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25) [single]

### Inferences
- **A safe taboo list for a host:**
  - not on Thursday
  - not in the waning moon (lunar days 16–30) if it can be avoided
  - not in cold months
  - purify the scissors first
  - touch with the wooden knife (or milk) before the steel
  - start right for a boy, left for a girl
  - go sunwise
  - never throw the hair away
  - braid or tidy the hair afterwards
  - keep alcohol modest
  - choose a first cutter with a "good life" (happily married, healthy, prosperous)
- **Sunwise (нар зөв) movement** is a general Mongolian ritual rule, and the sources apply it to how the child goes round the guests. Going against the sun would break it. This is my inference; no source states it as a taboo for this rite.

### Gaps
- No source found on: unlucky numbers of guests or gifts; a crying child (whether it is a bad omen); not giving knives or scissors as gifts; excluding guests of clashing (харш) animal years; pregnant women; or people in mourning attending. These may exist in oral practice but are unsourced.

## 8. Order of the whole day

### Takeaway
The traditional order:
1. Gather relatives in the morning.
2. Make the incense or milk offering.
3. Seat the guests, with the child at the head and the ivee person at the right-rear.
4. **The core rite:** announcement, first cut, milk, blessing, then guests cut sunwise with gifts.
5. Serve the essential **цагаалга** (milk-rice with dried curd and raisins) to everyone, with a bowl for each child.
6. Feast.
7. Long blessings (ерөөл), songs and music.
8. In the countryside, small wrestling and horse races.

Sources disagree on whether the цагаалга is tasted before or after the cutting. Today the event is usually a restaurant party run by an MC.

### Cited Findings
- **Order A (cut first, then feast):** "хүүхдийн үс даахь авах, өрөвлөг үргээх ёсон дууссаны дараа зочдод идээ цагаа барьж найр эхэлнэ" (after the hair rite ends, food is offered and the feast begins). Snippet only, from a search summary. — [updown.mn/55761](https://updown.mn/55761.html). Also: "ёслол найрын идээ зоог барьж, ёслолд ирсэн хүүхэд бүхэнд найрын хувь хүртээнэ" (after the rite, the feast food is served and every child present gets a share). — [updown.mn/80564](https://updown.mn/80564.html)
- **Order B (taste the цагаалга first, then cut):** guests taste the цагаалга first to open the feast, then the leader announces the haircut. — [24tsag.mn 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553). The ikon.mn advertorial lists: gather, set the incense offering, hair rite, gifts and blessings, then guests taste the цагаа and the feast begins. — [ikon.mn 2022](https://ikon.mn/n/2gs3)
- The dictionary version: set the table, serve food (the цагаалга is required; everyone tastes it and every child gets a bowl), then the leader announces the rite. — [ugiinbichig.mn](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi); [namurana](http://namurana.blogspot.com/2011/08/blog-post_6221.html)
- **Time:** gather in the morning or before noon. — [zindaa.mn](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); [updown.mn](https://updown.mn/80564.html) [multi]
- **Opening offering:** set an incense offering (сан) and sprinkle tea or milk (цацал) before starting. — [updown.mn](https://updown.mn/80564.html); [ikon.mn 2022](https://ikon.mn/n/2gs3) [multi]
- **Airag toast:** at the start, airag is offered (айраг сөн барих) and a blessing-reciter (ерөөлч) recites. Snippet only. — search summary of [gogo.mn](https://gogo.mn/r/jwol1). The dictionary version mentions "архин сөн барьж" (offering the first drink) before the feast. — [namurana](http://namurana.blogspot.com/2011/08/blog-post_6221.html)
- **After the rite:** songs and music ("дуу хуур болно"). — [ugiinbichig.mn](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi). Wrestling and horse races (a small naadam, a games festival) in rural areas. — [zindaa.mn](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); [24tsag.mn 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755); [medeelelzui](https://medeelelzui.blogmn.net/44325/huuhdiin-daahi-urgeeh-yos.html) [multi]
- **Guests arriving over the day:** in a 2014 home ceremony during Tsagaan Sar, guests came through the day and each cut on arrival; the full shave was done at the end of the day. Some families instead hold a formal banquet with all guests present from the start. — [drvalfarmer 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html) [single]
- **Modern additions:** professional photographers, food receptions and modern venues. — [JECS 2025](https://www.jecs.pl/index.php/jecs/article/view/1790). Restaurants are chosen for space, music, a stage or screen, set menus and professional organisers. — [huree.mn 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/) [multi]
- Some families replace the traditional first-birthday celebration with other parties. Modern songs and blessings are replacing traditional ones. — [Nomadic Studies 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25) [single]

### Inferences
- **A practical "most common today" running order** for a restaurant event. This is my synthesis of the sources; the timings are illustrative and not from any source.
  1. Guests arrive and are greeted. The child is in a deel at the head table, the ivee-year cutter at the honoured seat.
  2. The MC welcomes everyone and explains the rite. Optionally the opening цагаалга or milk is tasted, and the airag or first drink is offered.
  3. **Core rite:** announcement ("За одоо …-ийн даахийг үргээе"); the wooden-knife touch; the first cut with the khadag-tied scissors (right temple for a boy, left for a girl); milk to the child and a dab on the forehead; a blessing and gift from the first cutter.
  4. Guests come up in turn, sunwise or by age: a snip, a short blessing, a gift. The mother gathers the hair into the khadag. A lock is left for anyone missing.
  5. Formal ерөөл (long blessing verse) by an elder or a hired ерөөлч.
  6. Feast (цагаалга served to all, a bowl for each child), songs, and games or a show for the children.
  7. Closing words of thanks by the parents. The full shave or trim happens later.
- **Return gifts (хариу бэлэг)** to guests are not covered in my sources. Another researcher may cover them.

### Gaps
- No source on **return gifts** to guests, or on a formal **closing** ritual.
- No source settles whether the цагаалга is tasted before or after the cutting. Both orders exist in reputable books (Family A vs the dictionary family).
- Full blessing texts were not collected here. They are at the [huree.mn blessing page](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D2%AF%D1%81%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B9-%D0%BD%D0%B0%D0%B9%D1%80%D0%BD%D1%8B-%D0%B5%D1%80%D3%A9%D3%A9%D0%BB-%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD/) and [mnb.mn](https://www.mnb.mn/i/229335), which I did not open.
