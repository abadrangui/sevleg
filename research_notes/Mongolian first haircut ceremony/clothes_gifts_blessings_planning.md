# Mongolian First Haircut Ceremony (Үсний найр / Сэвлэг үргээх ёслол): Clothes, Gifts, Blessings, Etiquette and Modern Planning

In short: Mongolian sources agree on the core: the child is dressed up and seated at the head of the table, each guest cuts a lock, says a blessing (ерөөл) and gives a gift, and the hair is kept in a khadag (хадаг, a ceremonial silk scarf; usually blue in practice). Today most city families hold it in a restaurant with an MC (хөтлөгч, host/master of ceremonies) and set menus. Writers criticise big "everyone invited" parties and alcohol. I found no reliable source for 2025–2026 cash-gift amounts by relationship, for return gifts to guests, or for boy/girl clothing colours.

Notes on words used below: даахь / сэвлэг = a baby's first, never-cut hair. Ивээл жил = the zodiac year that "protects" the child's birth year; a person born in that year is often asked to cut first. Хоймор / баруун хойно = the honour seat at the back (north-west) of a ger. Цагаалга = sweet milk rice with curd and raisins, the one dish that must be served. Ерөөлч = a person who recites blessings. All prices are approximate and dated to the source.

## 1. Clothing: what do the child, parents and guests wear, and what does it cost?

### Takeaway
Sources only say the child is "dressed beautifully" (often in a new deel) and seated at the head of the table; I found no rule for boy vs girl colours, no parent or guest dress code, and no deel-rental offers. Children's deels in Ulaanbaatar cost about 55,000–120,000₮ in 2024–2025 (ready-made), with boots, hats and belts extra.

### Cited Findings
- The child is "dressed beautifully" ("хүүгээ гоё хувцаслан") and seated at the head of the table ("ширээний тэргүүнд суулган") on the morning of the chosen day. — [Huree.mn, 2024-01-17](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/); same wording in [Updown.mn, 2018-08-21](https://updown.mn/55761.html) and [Zindaa.mn, 2018-02-22](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)
- "The child is dressed neatly, sometimes in a new deel." — [Mongolian Store (undated, English)](https://mongolianstore.com/tradition-of-first-hair-cutting-ceremony/)
- The child wears "fancy clothing as an important person of the day." — [Geru Travel (undated, English)](https://gerutravel.com/hair-cutting-ceremony-of-children/)
- Personal account: "The child's father carves the wooden knife himself, while the mother sews the child's traditional deel (Mongolian robe) for the occasion." — [Borderless Journal, Odbayar Dorj, 2025-04-14](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/)
- Old taboo: in early times it was taboo to dress children in a deel with patterns ("эрт дээр үед хүүхдэд хээ хуар бүхий дээл өмсөхийг цээрлэдэг байжээ"). — [Ergelt.mn, 2024-01-23](https://ergelt.mn/news/125/single/41227)
- Hair after the ceremony: Mongolians avoided leaving hair loose and tangled (thought to shorten life). So the child's remaining hair is braided and the end tied with camel-wool thread, a leather strap or a tassel ("тэмээний ноосон утас, сур юмуу цацгаар боодог"). — [Huree.mn, 2024-01-17](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/); [Updown.mn, 2018-08-21](https://updown.mn/55761.html)
- Old style: a tuft ("тэвэг") was left on boys and two tufts ("2 хөхөл") on girls, meaning the boy would become a man of the state and the girl a mother of a home. — [24tsag.mn, 2020-10-19](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755)
- Guests: one foreign guest wrote "We are dressed in our deels because the haircutting ceremony took place during the Tsaagan Sar holidays" (suggests deel for guests is linked to the holiday, not a rule). — [Musings in Mongolia blog, 2014-02](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html)
- Prices, Ulaanbaatar, January 2024 (before Tsagaan Sar): children's (0–12 years) fashion deels at Narantuul market 60,000–90,000₮; "Borgoljin" brand children's deel 55,000–85,000₮, children's vest (хантааз) 85,000₮; "Iveel" brand children's Mongolian shirt 75,000–85,000₮; belts from 25,000₮ (women's "Daalin" belts 60,000–95,000₮; men's cloth/silk belts 35,000–120,000₮); hair bag (үсний уут) 15,000–25,000₮. Adult deels: Narantuul women's from 180,000₮ up to 650,000₮, men's 220,000–780,000₮; "Tumen Torgo" 299,000–999,000₮; premium "Dörvön Arvait" women's 1.5–2.5 million₮, men's 1.5–3.5 million₮, couple set (хос дээл) 4–5 million₮, its hat 700,000₮ and boots 400,000₮. — [Ergelt.mn, 2024-01-23](https://ergelt.mn/news/125/single/41227)
- Prices, February 2025: an opinion piece says a children's deel can cost 120,000₮ and children's felt boots (эсгий улавч) 120,000–130,000₮, and complains about poor fabric quality (shiny synthetic fabric that tears in a few days) and that an adult deel bought for 400,000₮ may not survive to the next Tsagaan Sar. — [News.mn, 2025-02-18](https://news.mn/r/2783989/)
- Online shop: "Nandin" children's brand silk deel (size for an 11-year-old girl) listed on Gerege shop; a search-result snippet showed about 104,999₮ (price not visible in my own page download; date unknown). — [Gerege shop](https://shop.gerege.mn/product/259/)
- Older price point (July 2019, Naadam season): children's Mongolian shirts and children's boots from about 20,000₮ upward at Narantuul; women's silk fabric 5,000–35,000₮ (unit not stated); sewn belts 20,000–25,000₮; tailors scarce and expensive. — [Ikon.mn, 2019-07-04](https://ikon.mn/n/1m7t)

### Inferences
- A practical ready-made child outfit in Ulaanbaatar (deel + belt + simple boots/hat) likely costs roughly 100,000–300,000₮ in 2025–2026 at market/brand-store level, and much more from premium brands. This is my estimate from the 2024–2025 figures above, not a quoted price.
- Because the old custom was that the mother sews the deel, a home-sewn deel is the "traditional" choice; ready-made or brand deels are the modern norm.
- Choosing a plain (not heavily patterned) deel for a small child matches the old taboo noted by Ergelt.mn.

### Gaps
- No source found that names a special colour for boys vs girls at this ceremony.
- No source found on what parents should wear, or a guest dress code (beyond one anecdote).
- No source found on deel rental for children, matching family deels for this event, or "themed outfits". Searches for "дээл түрээс хүүхэд" returned nothing relevant.
- No 2026 price survey found; newest figures are Jan 2024 (Ergelt) and Feb 2025 (News.mn).

## 2. Gifts from guests: what is given, how much, and what is taboo?

### Takeaway
Traditionally a guest "promised" livestock (a white sheep with a blaze, a lamb, sometimes a foal); today guests give toys, stationery, hair ribbons, badges, sweets and money, each given at the moment the guest cuts a lock and says a blessing. I found no reliable 2025–2026 source on how much cash to give by relationship.

### Cited Findings
- Traditional gift: to "anoint the child's head" ("толгойг мялааж") guests promised a white sheep with a white face blaze or a lamb ("халзан цагаан хонь юмуу хурга амладаг байжээ"). Modern gifts: toys, pencils, stationery, hair ribbons, badges, sweets and pastries, money ("тоглоом, харандаа, бичгийн хэрэглэл, үсний тууз, тэмдэг, чихэр боов, мөнгө төгрөг"). — [Huree.mn, 2024-01-17](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/); [Updown.mn, 2018-08-21](https://updown.mn/55761.html); [Zindaa.mn, 2018-02-22](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)
- Another list: hair ribbon, badge, postcard, sweets/pastries, or a promise of livestock ("үсний тууз, тэмдэг, ил захидал, чихэр боов, мал амлаж болно"). — [Updown.mn, 2018-06-08](https://updown.mn/50592.html)
- Mongolians long promised sheep and livestock to a child as an auspicious act ("хүүхдэд халуун хошуутай хэмээн бэлэгшээж хонь, малыг амладаг байсан"); some families held a small naadam the same day with foal races and wrestling. — [24tsag.mn, 2020-10-19](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755)
- Timing: "while the child's hair is being cut, everyone gives the child a gift" and says a blessing ("даахь /өрөвлөг/ үргээж байх үедээ хүн бүр хүүхдэд бэлэг өгч ... ерөөлийн үг хэлдэг"). — [24tsag.mn, 2020-10-19](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755); [Ikon.mn (sponsored), 2022-02-16](https://ikon.mn/n/2gs3)
- Anecdotes from foreign guests in Ulaanbaatar:
  - Gift is "usually cash ($5 – $20), but sometimes a toy"; "Next to money, toy trucks were the most common gifts." — [Live from UB blog, 2012-05-17](https://livefromub.wordpress.com/2012/05/17/a-mongolian-hair-cutting-ceremony/)
  - Gift is "usually money (symbol of good fortune) but it could be a toy," followed by a wish or blessing. — [Musings in Mongolia, 2014-02](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html)
  - At a party of "close to 100 people," gifts ranged from small toys to "child-sized battery powered cars" and "a nice bike," plus money. — [Bran's in Mongolia blog, 2014-10](https://bransnmongolia.blogspot.com/2014/10/hair-cutting-ceremony.html)
  - Each guest cut a lock, put it in a little bag, sipped milk tea and gave it to the child, then "handed the kid some money." — [Min in Mongolia blog, 2014-08-12](https://mininmongolia.wordpress.com/2014/08/12/the-hair-cutting-ceremony-and-rug-washing-day/)
- "Gifts may include clothing, toys, books, money or livestock in pastoral families. The value of the gift matters less than the relationship it represents." "A modest child-appropriate gift or money is common. Ask the parents when in doubt." — [Mongolian Store (undated)](https://mongolianstore.com/tradition-of-first-hair-cutting-ceremony/)
- Search-result snippets (pages not reachable for me to verify) said: an invited guest "must come with at least a small gift," usually two plates of dairy food called "гэрийн дээж, найрын дээж"; and gifts can include clothes, shoes, silk, cloth, coins, plus a promised sheep or foal. Likely sources: [Ikhmongol.mn](http://ikhmongol.mn/show/5107), [Mongolmedleg.org](https://mongolmedleg.org/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D0%B4%D0%B0%D0%B0%D1%85%D1%8C-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BE%D0%BD/) — UNVERIFIED.
- General Mongolian gift-taboo lists (not specific to this ceremony) say not to give knives, forks or other sharp objects as gifts; if someone insists, the receiver "buys" it with a small coin. — search-result summary of [Zuv.mn](https://zuv.mn/n/e1n) and [Caak.mn](https://www.caak.mn/post/view/439164b6-b95b-4f34-aa59-54f71800e1dd) — not opened by me; popular-belief listicles, low reliability.

### Inferences
- The most "correct" traditional-style gift is a promise of livestock (mainly in herding families); in the city, cash plus a small toy or school item is the norm.
- Since the ceremony itself uses scissors and a wooden knife, the sharp-object taboo is about gifts, not the ritual tools. Avoiding knives/scissors as gifts is a safe choice.
- The 2012 "US$5–20" range is far too old to use for 2025–2026 amounts.

### Gaps
- No reliable source found for typical 2025–2026 cash amounts by relationship (grandparents, close relatives, friends, coworkers). Mongolian searches ("үсний найрт хэдэн төгрөг өгөх", "найранд хэдэн төгрөг хийх") returned nothing usable. This likely lives in Facebook groups and word of mouth.
- No source found on gold/silver items or opening savings accounts as gifts for this ceremony.
- No source found on a "хишиг" (blessed share) gift specific to this ceremony.

## 3. How gifts and the khadag are presented

### Takeaway
The khadag's main role in this ceremony is ritual: it is tied to the scissors handle, and the cut hair is wrapped in it and kept. Foreign eyewitnesses describe it as blue. The gift is handed over when each guest cuts a lock and says a blessing. I found no source that says guests must present money on a khadag at this event.

### Cited Findings
- The scissors have a khadag tied to the handle ("бариулд нь хадаг уясан хайчаар"); the first lock is wrapped in a khadag; then each guest cuts a lock and it is added to the khadag and kept ("хадганд нэмэн боож хадгална"). — [Huree.mn, 2024-01-17](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/); [Zindaa.mn, 2018-02-22](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)
- The mother collects the cut hair ("эх нь сэвийг нь авч хадганд боодог"). — [Updown.mn, 2018-06-08](https://updown.mn/50592.html)
- The kept hair is used as folk medicine (e.g. burnt and smelled to stop a nosebleed). — [Updown.mn, 2018-06-08](https://updown.mn/50592.html)
- Colour, eyewitness accounts: "A knot was tied in one end of a blue khadag as a depository for the locks... The scissors were tied to the other end." — [Musings in Mongolia, 2014-02](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html); "Scissors with a blue scarf (a prayer scarf) attached" — [Bran's in Mongolia, 2014-10](https://bransnmongolia.blogspot.com/2014/10/hair-cutting-ceremony.html); "khadag (traditional sacred blue cloth)" — [Live from UB, 2012-05-17](https://livefromub.wordpress.com/2012/05/17/a-mongolian-hair-cutting-ceremony/)
- Khadag colour meanings: blue (цэнхэр) = eternity and peace, the sky; used for greeting each other and relatives. White (цагаан) = pure mind, generosity; a very high honour, offered to Buddhas and to the most honoured elders. Yellow = for one's religious teacher only. Green and red = special Buddhist rites, not given to people. — [Eguur.mn (undated)](https://eguur.mn/656176/)
- How to present a khadag: fold it lengthwise three times and hold it with the open side facing the receiver ("амыг барих хүний өөдөөс нь харуулж"); the receiver takes it with both hands. Khadag use is listed for "даахь үргээх" among other rites. — [Mongolian Wikipedia, "Хадаг"](https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A5%D0%B0%D0%B4%D0%B0%D0%B3)
- A professional blessing-reciter's text opens with the reciter "holding up my great khadag" ("Нанжин их хадагаа бариад намайг ерөө гэлээ" / "Намирсан их хадгаа дэлгээд"), i.e. the ерөөлч holds a khadag while blessing. — [Creative Mongolia (undated)](https://cm.nmma.co/135444/); [Updown.mn, 2018-08-21](https://updown.mn/55761.html)
- Order of cutting: a family member or honoured guest cuts first, then the child is led sunwise (clockwise) past parents, elders and guests ("нар зөв тойруулж"). — [Huree.mn, 2024-01-17](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/); family members first, then other guests — [Bran's in Mongolia, 2014-10](https://bransnmongolia.blogspot.com/2014/10/hair-cutting-ceremony.html); "closest family and friends are first" — [Live from UB, 2012](https://livefromub.wordpress.com/2012/05/17/a-mongolian-hair-cutting-ceremony/)

### Inferences
- A blue khadag is the safe default for the scissors and the hair bundle. A white khadag fits an offering to the most honoured elder or the person who cuts first, based on the colour meanings above.
- Cash is likely handed in an envelope at the moment of cutting, but I found no source describing envelope vs khadag for cash.

### Gaps
- No Mongolian source says which khadag colour guests should bring, or whether guests present a khadag at all at this ceremony.

## 4. Return gifts from hosts (бэлэг барих / буцаах бэлэг)

### Takeaway
I found almost nothing on formal return gifts. Sources only say hosts feed every guest, give each visiting child a bowl of цагаалга, and give a gift to an uninvited passer-by who happens to arrive (seen as a lucky sign).

### Cited Findings
- Guests start to eat and drink once the child's hair has been touched ("Хүүхдийн сэвлэгэнд хүрч эхэлснээр идээ ундаагаа ирсэн зочид хүртэж эхэлдэг"). — [Updown.mn, 2018-06-08](https://updown.mn/50592.html)
- Everyone tastes the цагаалга and every child who came gets a bowl ("ирсэн хүүхэд бүгдэд аяга аягаар өгч будаалдаг"). — [Updown.mn, 2018-06-08](https://updown.mn/50592.html); [Washyoku/Govi Mirage (undated)](http://washyoku.tavanbogd.mn/index/index/news/%D0%A1%D0%AD%D0%92%D0%9B%D0%AD%D0%93%20%D2%AE%D0%A0%D0%93%D0%AD%D0%AD%D0%A5%20%D0%81%D0%A1%D0%9E%D0%9D)
- An uninvited person who happens to arrive is seen as a good sign: hosts invite them in, seat them high, feed them and send them off with a gift ("уриагүй боловч хэргийн учирт санаамсаргүй тохиолдсон хүнийг ерөөлтэй байж хэмээн ихэд бэлгэшээн дээш нь урьж, дайлж цайлаад бэлэг өгч явуулдаг заншилтай"). — [Mglnews.mn (undated)](http://www.mglnews.mn/content/read/58281.htm)
- The guest who holds the child's protective year (ивээл жилтэй хүн) sits at the north-west (baruun khoino) and "becomes the most honoured person of this family." — [Huree.mn, 2024-01-17](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/)

### Inferences
- If hosts want to give something back, the most tradition-consistent items are food (a share of the feast, цагаалга for children) and a special gift or khadag for the first cutter / ивээл жилтэй guest, because sources single that person out for honour. This is my inference, not a sourced custom.

### Gaps
- No source found on specific return gifts to elders or to the person who cuts first, or on modern souvenirs (e.g. printed cups, favours). Searches "даахь үргээх дурсгалын бэлэг зочдод", "үсний найр зочдод бэлэг барих" returned nothing on this.

## 5. Blessings (ерөөл), songs and praise poems: full texts

### Takeaway
There is one widely republished traditional haircut blessing (published by "Тусгал" newspaper and copied by Updown, 24tsag, Huree and others); its fullest copy is below, plus a long variant from Creative Mongolia and a short, printable list of wishes. A modern song "Сэвлэг үргээх ерөөлийн дуу" (2023) exists with named lyricist and composer.

### Cited Findings

#### Text 1. Short spoken formula (most common)
Said while the child tastes milk from a cup after the first cut:

> Урт наслан, удаан жарга
>
> Урт настай, удаан жаргалтай, аавдаа ачтай, ээждээ тустай, төр улсдаа зүтгэлтэй, түмэн олны манлай болж яваарай

Meaning: "Live long, be happy for a long time. Be long-lived and happy, a help to your father and mother, devoted to your country, and a leader among the people."
Source: [Zindaa.mn, 2018-02-22](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); [Huree.mn, 2024-01-17](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/); [Updown.mn, 2018-08-21](https://updown.mn/55761.html) (which writes "Урт насалж, удаан жарга"). Author: traditional (no author).

#### Text 2. Short guest wish
> аавдаа ачлалтай, ээждээ энэрэлтэй, урт насалж, удаан жаргаж элбэг баян амьдраарай

Meaning: "Be grateful to your father, kind to your mother, live long, be happy long, and live in plenty."
Source: [Updown.mn, 2018-06-08](https://updown.mn/50592.html). Traditional.

#### Text 3. Guest greeting phrases (spoken)
> Том хүн болоорой! / Сайн хүн болоорой!

Meaning: "Grow into a big person! / Become a good person!" (reported in Latin letters as "Tom xun bolooroi!" / "Sain xun bolooroi!").
Source: [Live from UB, 2012-05-17](https://livefromub.wordpress.com/2012/05/17/a-mongolian-hair-cutting-ceremony/).

#### Text 4. "Хүүхдийн үс үргээхэд хэлэх ерөөл" — full traditional text, version A (as published by "Тусгал")
> Эрхэм дүү …………………. –ний Энхрий бяцхан хүүгийхээ  
> Олон жилээр ургуулсан  
> Он удаанаар энхрийлсэн  
> Онгон хонгор даахийг нь  
> Одод баясган авсны  
> Найр цэнгэл баяр бэлэг болгож  
> Намирсан их хадгаа дэлгээд  
> Нандин идээний дээжийг өргөөд  
> Найрын олны өмнө  
> Намайг бэлэг барь гэлээ.  
> Энхрий бяцхан хүүгийн  
> Оройн сэв нь  
> Онгон буурал болж  
> Хөхлийн сэв нь  
> Цал буурал болж

### Inferences
- Texts 4–8 are versions of one folk blessing. Text 5 (Тусгал version B) is the most complete alcohol-free version and the best choice for a reciter at a family event. Text 1 and Text 9 suit ordinary guests.
- The traditional texts use "хүү" (son). For a girl, families commonly swap in "охин" (daughter); I found no published girl-specific version.

### Gaps
- No full blessing found that is written for a girl.
- No source confirms "Ээжийн бүүвэй" (or any other specific lullaby) as a standard haircut song.
- No haircut-specific "магтаал" (praise poem) text found.
- Original Тусгал article and the S.Dulam (folklorist) interview on tusgal.mn were unreachable.

## 6. Etiquette rules for hosts and guests

### Takeaway
Invite few, carefully chosen people (close kin, respected elders, the person of the child's protective year, people of good character); hold it in the morning; seat the most honoured guest at the north-west honour place; no hard alcohol; no quarrels; lead the child sunwise; guests bless, cut a small lock and give a gift in turn.

### Cited Findings
- Who to invite: siblings, relatives, respected people, and the person of the child's protective year ("ивээл жилтэй хүн"). — [Huree.mn, 2024-01-17](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/); [Zindaa.mn, 2018-02-22](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)
- The child's paternal and maternal uncles (авга, нагац) "must be present." — [Updown.mn, 2018-06-08](https://updown.mn/50592.html)
- Traditionally only a small feast with the protective-year person, close relatives, and people with "complete" families ("үр хүүхэд нь өв тэгш хүмүүс"). The article criticises inviting all relatives, friends and coworkers to touch the child's head, since "you do not know" if a thief, cheat, greedy or quarrelsome person is among them. — [Updown.mn, 2018-06-08](https://updown.mn/50592.html); same text in [Mglnews.mn](http://www.mglnews.mn/content/read/58281.htm), [Dayarmongol.mn](http://dayarmongol.mn/index.php?newsid=7086)
- Personal account (2025): "In Mongolian culture, divorced individuals are not invited to participate in a child's haircut ceremony," and the author questions this. — [Borderless Journal, 2025-04-14](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/)
- Who cuts first varies by group: among Khalkh, Dörvöd, Bayad, Myangad, Khotgoid, the grandfather, father or oldest honoured guest; among Buryats, the midwife who delivered the child. — [24tsag.mn, 2020-10-19](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755)
- Seating: the protective-year person sits at the north-west of the home, "the most honoured person" that day. — [Huree.mn, 2024-01-17](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/); at a 2014 restaurant party, the child sat at the head table with grandparents, aunts, uncles and special guests — [Bran's in Mongolia](https://bransnmongolia.blogspot.com/2014/10/hair-cutting-ceremony.html)
- Time: morning or before noon. Guests who arrive after noon are not allowed to touch the child's hair ("Үдээс хойш ирсэн зочдод хүүхдийнхээ үсэнд хүргэдэггүй"). — [Updown.mn, 2018-06-08](https://updown.mn/50592.html)
- Behaviour: family members must not speak angrily or quarrel ("ам муруйж болдоггүй") that day; do not invite many guests; never serve hard alcohol ("хатуу сархад") or stale food. Airag (fermented mare's milk) is allowed. Reason given: the rite honours a child's four "divine" qualities (no greed, no hoarding, no idea of dirt, no rank discrimination). — [Updown.mn, 2018-06-08](https://updown.mn/50592.html)
- Contradiction in practice: at a 2012 hotel-banquet ceremony in Ulaanbaatar, "ample vodka, beer and juice" were served and an emcee ran the evening. — [Live from UB, 2012-05-17](https://livefromub.wordpress.com/2012/05/17/a-mongolian-hair-cutting-ceremony/)
- Before touching the hair, scissors are purified with juniper and incense smoke ("арц, хүж уугиулан хайчаа ариулна"); the child is led sunwise past parents, elders and guests. — [Huree.mn, 2024-01-17](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/)
- Day choice: Thursday was avoided as "хүний хишиг өдөр"; Monday and Friday preferred by Khalkh. — [24tsag.mn, 2020-10-19](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755). Some areas avoid the 4th day. — [Huree.mn, 2024-01-17](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/)
- Guest tips (English site): offer a short, sincere blessing rather than imitating words you do not understand; do not take the hair or joke about it without permission; ask parents when unsure about the gift. — [Mongolian Store (undated)](https://mongolianstore.com/tradition-of-first-hair-cutting-ceremony/)
- Length: one Ulaanbaatar ceremony lasted about 3 hours. — [Live from UB, 2012](https://livefromub.wordpress.com/2012/05/17/a-mongolian-hair-cutting-ceremony/)
- Last guests may find the child tired and refusing the milk bowl. — [Min in Mongolia, 2014](https://mininmongolia.wordpress.com/2014/08/12/the-hair-cutting-ceremony-and-rug-washing-day/)

### Inferences
- A host who wants to follow tradition and avoid criticism should: keep the hair-cutting part to close family and chosen elders, start before noon, seat the protective-year guest or eldest at the honour place, serve airag/dairy rather than vodka during the rite, and have an elder or ерөөлч open with a blessing.
- Guests: arrive on time (before the cutting), wait to be called, cut only a small lock, say a short blessing, give the gift at that moment, and avoid arguments or drinking during the rite.

### Gaps
- No source on how many days/weeks ahead to invite, or who "must" be invited beyond uncles and the protective-year person.
- No source on how to greet on entering, or how long guests should stay.

## 7. Modern planning: venue, invitations, MC, photo/video, decorations, entertainment, budget, and criticism

### Takeaway
In recent years most city families hold the event in a restaurant that sells a package (set menu per person, stage, sound, MC, sometimes blessing-reciter and morin khuur). Per-person menus were about 78,000–135,000₮ (children ~60,000₮) on 2022–2024 listings. Writers criticise big parties, drinking and rising costs. I found no published 2025–2026 total-budget figure.

### Cited Findings
- Trend: "In recent years most families hold the child's haircut ceremony in a restaurant." Listed benefits: large space; music and microphone; set menus; stage and screen for greetings and performances; professional cooks and organisers, which saves the family time. Choose a restaurant that accepts this ceremony and keeps national traditions; "the MC (хөтлөгч) will be the main driving force" of the event. — [Huree.mn, 2024-01-17](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/)

- Social cost: a 2013 column lists haircut feasts, weddings, birthdays and work-day parties as part of "endless celebrations," saying some people mark ten celebrations a month and the cost "grows every year." — [Shuud.mn, 2013-09-26](https://www.shuud.mn/a/284447)

### Inferences
- Rough restaurant budget (my estimate, not a sourced figure): 50 adults × 90,000–135,000₮ + 15 children × 60,000₮ ≈ 5.4–7.6 million₮ for food alone at 2022–2024 menu prices; add MC, photo/video (~0.2–0.8 million₮), invitations, decor and outfits. A home event with close family can cost a fraction of this.
- Package deals that include a ерөөлч and morin khuur let families keep the traditional core inside a modern venue.

### Gaps

- No current price for an ordinary (non-celebrity) MC for a haircut party.
- No price data for balloon/theme decor or kids' entertainers.
- No surveys or recent (2023–2026) news articles found on over-spending debates specific to "үсний найр"; the critiques found are from 2013–2018 (and undated reposts).

## 8. Published checklists or timelines from planners or parenting sites

### Takeaway
I found no full checklist or timeline. Only short tips exist (choose a lucky day; pick a restaurant that accepts the ceremony; focus on food and the MC).

### Cited Findings
- Restaurant-choice tips: a venue that accepts this ceremony, ideally one that keeps national traditions; pay attention to food; the MC is the main driver. — [Huree.mn, 2024-01-17](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/)
- Lucky-day lists (lunar days such as the 3rd, 8th, 15th, 27th) are published by many sites as the first planning step. — [Updown.mn, 2018-06-08](https://updown.mn/50592.html); [Huree.mn](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/)

### Inferences
- A practical order from the sources: (1) pick the lucky day with an astrologer/lama; (2) choose the protective-year person and the first cutter; (3) set the guest list (small for the cutting); (4) book venue/MC/ерөөлч; (5) send invitations; (6) prepare deel, khadag, scissors, wooden knife, milk cup, цагаалга; (7) on the day, start before noon. This is my synthesis.

- No Mongolian event-planner or parenting-site checklist with lead times (e.g. "book venue 2 months ahead") was found.
