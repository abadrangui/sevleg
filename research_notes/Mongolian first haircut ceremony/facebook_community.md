# What Mongolian Facebook users say about the child's first haircut ceremony (Сэвлэг үргээх ёслол / Үсний найр / Даахь үргээх), 2025–2026

**Please read first.** Everything here is personal opinion and experience from Facebook posts and comments. It is not an authority on tradition. People often disagree, and many "rules" are repeated without a source. Counts like "seen in ~6 posts" are only my count of the posts and comments I could load. They are not a survey.

**Method (how I collected this).** The research was done on 2026-10-11 in the user's own Chrome. I used one tab and waited 5–10 seconds between page loads. I did read-only actions only: search, scroll, open a post, expand "See more", and change the comment sort to "All comments" once. I opened and fully read about 27 posts with their comments. I also saw about 20 more posts as search-result previews. Most posts are from Aug–Oct 2026, and a few are from 2025.
- Main source: the public group **"Сэвлэг үргээх ёсон"** (about 101.4K members, based in Bayanzürkh, Ulaanbaatar): https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/ . Members can post anonymously, so many authors show as "Anonymous participant" or random animal names. Many comments are vendor ads.
- Other groups I found: "Ээжүүдийн групп 🇲🇳" (246K). Its search only returned unrelated story posts, so it was not useful. There are also vendor-type groups such as "Үсний найр, 1 насны Төрсөн өдрийн хоол захиалга ширээ засалт" (2.5K) and "Хүлээн авалт [Төрсөн өдөр, Үсний найр, Хурим найр]" (1.2K). I did not read these in depth.
- Limits: for a non-member, the group's main feed loaded only one post, so I used the group's own search box instead, which worked. Facebook loaded only part of each comment thread (the "Most relevant" view), so long threads were only partly read.
- **Groups joined: none.** The coordinator said joining was allowed, but every group I needed was public and readable without joining.
- **Warnings or checkpoints: none.** I saw no captcha, security check, "slow down" or "temporarily blocked" message.
- Privacy: I did not record any names or phone numbers of private people. I only name businesses and public groups.

## 1. What age is "right" for boys and girls? What do people disagree about?

### Takeaway
The most common rule on Facebook uses **хий нас** ("lunar age", which counts the time in the womb, so it is about one year more than the real age). By that count, girls have it at an even age (2 or 4) and boys at an odd age (3 or 5). But people often mix up хий нас and real age, so the same rule gets stated in opposite ways. A strong minority view, citing a folk-culture lecturer, says **both sexes should be done at an odd age, mainly 5**, and calls "girls at an even age" a modern mistake.

### Cited Findings
- A commenter said that in old times boys and girls were both done at 3 or 5, and that "later" it became girls even and boys odd. They advised showing the child's details to a lama because each family differs. — [Group post, Jul 31 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2293742201425272/)
- Same thread: "girls at хий нас 2 or 4, boys at 3 or 5". Another reply said "even, by хий нас". — [Group post, Jul 31 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2293742201425272/)
- **Regional difference** (1 commenter): "Khalkha people take girls' hair at an even age and boys' at an odd age; in the western aimags (provinces) it is the reverse." — [Group post, Jul 31 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2293742201425272/)
- One thread on "хий нас vs real age" (~June 2026) shows the confusion. One person said "girl at real age 3, which is хий нас 4; boy at real age 2 or 4, which is хий нас 3 or 5", but described this as "girl odd, boy even". Others corrected it: "girls even by хий нас, boys odd by хий нас". One person explained хий нас with an example: a child born after Feb 2022 (tiger year) is хий нас 5 but real age 4 in 2026. — [Group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2237105633755596/)
- The group's own explainer post (Jul 31 2025, 81 shares) says boys at 3 or 5 (арга, odd) in deer-rut season and girls at 2 or 4 (билиг, even) in cuckoo season. It also says "some areas do it at the first birthday". A commenter asked "what is the source?" and got no answer. — [Group post, Jul 31 2025](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/1980054656127363/)
- A long post in the same group disagrees. Citing a lecture by the folk researcher С. Дамбий (МУСГЗ), it says to cut when the child can say "чулуу" ("stone") clearly, at an **odd age for boys and girls alike**, in an ивээл year (a zodiac year that "protects" the child), mainly at 5. It calls "girls at an even age" a mistake spread over the last ~50 years, "even by today's lamas". It says even ages were used only quietly for sickly children or families who had lost children. — [Group post, date not shown (~mid-2026)](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2274331730032986/)
- Season: girls in spring ("when the cuckoo sings"), boys in autumn ("when deer call"). People note that many girls are now done in autumn anyway. — [Group post, Jul 31 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2293742201425272/); [Group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2274331730032986/)
- Age questions come up often. Examples: "at what age for a girl?" (Sept 13 2026), "tiger-year girl born Jan 2023 – can we do it on the 28th?" (8 comments), "rabbit-year boy born Sept 2023 – what age?", "rabbit boy born Nov 2023 – grandparents' lama said Oct 3", and "3y3m girl with late speech – did cutting help?". — [1](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2334810863985072/), [2](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2345558206243671/), [3](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2327461184720040/), [4](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2311962319603260/), [5](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2301008084032017/)

### Inferences
- A lot of the "even vs odd" fight is really about counting method. "Girl at хий нас 4" and "girl at real age 3 (odd)" can describe the same child. A guide should tell readers to say clearly which age they mean.
- Most parents finally follow a lama's or grandparents' advice for their own child, not a general rule.

### Gaps
- I found no real Facebook data on Buryat, Dörvöd, Kazakh or Dariganga age rules. The only regional claim was "western aimags do the reverse", from one commenter with no source.
- The comments on the "odd age for all" post did not load, so I could not see how people reacted to it.

## 2. How do families choose the day (lama, зурхай, calendar apps)?

### Takeaway
Most families **ask a lama**, giving the child's birth-year animal (and sometimes the birth month and day). Different lamas often give different dates for the same zodiac sign, which confuses parents. Some families use Google or online "good hair-cutting day" calendars. Morning time slots (about 9:30–13:30) are standard.

### Cited Findings
- In the October 2026 thread "which days in October are good?" (30 comments), many parents shared lama-given dates by zodiac. A dragon-year boy got Oct 21. Tiger-year boys got Oct 15, Oct 18 ("very good day"), Oct 14 & 21, or Oct 13, from different lamas. Another lama said only Oct 4 for a dragon boy, because "after the 11th the month is incompatible (харш)". — [Group post, ~early Oct 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2349832365816255/)
- Same thread, the warnings: "Oct 15 is an ивээл day but a 'black dog' (хар нохой) day, also bad – ask the lama well". "The 13th is the second (repeated) day, so we chose the 18th." "Besides the birth year, the birth month and day matter too." One parent asked, "why are the answers so different?" — [Group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2349832365816255/)
- The time of the first touch: two families were each given **9:40–11:40** on Oct 18 for a tiger-year boy. — [Group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2349832365816255/)
- People book the ерөөлч and хуурч for morning slots, for example "8/26, between 9:30 and 13:30". — [Group post, ~Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2290836038382555/)
- A couple chose the date from a Google "good hair-cut day" calendar without asking a lama. It later turned out to match the child's ивээл month and day. Another parent said "I don't even know which lama to ask". — [Group post, Aug 14 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2306690880130404/)
- Lunar-day lists shared in group explainers: good days are the 3rd, 4th, 8th, 10th, 11th, 13th, 14th, 15th, 19th, 22nd, 23rd and 27th (one post), plus the 6th and 26th (another post). Mon/Wed/Fri are good. Good animal-days are mouse, ox, sheep, monkey, dog and pig. The child's ивээл month or day is also good. "Grandparents say the child chooses the day itself." — [Group post, Jul 31 2025](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/1980054656127363/); [Group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2274331730032986/)
- One explainer says guests who arrive after noon should not touch the hair. Another says to do it in the morning, before noon. — [Group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2274331730032986/); [Group post, Jul 31 2025](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/1980054656127363/)
- A tiger-year boy family planned Sept 26 because "Oct 13 is a good day, but it's the second day, so they say avoid it" (28 comments). — [Group post, Sept 14 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2334234204042738/)

### Inferences
- "Ask a lama" is the norm (seen in ~6 threads). But parents get conflicting dates, which is a clear pain point. A tool that explains *why* a day is good or bad (зурхай, the Mongolian astrology calendar: ивээл, хар нохой, repeated days) would help.

### Gaps
- No post named a specific calendar app. "Google" was the only digital tool mentioned.

## 3. What families actually did: steps, who cut first, what was said

### Takeaway
A typical 2026 ceremony goes like this. A hired **ерөөлч** (blessing speaker), often together with a **морин хуурч** (horse-head fiddle player), opens with music and a blessing. The **ивээл-year person** tastes milk and touches the hair first. Then parents, grandparents and relatives cut by seniority, then guests, each saying a blessing and giving a gift. Finally the child is shaved fully with a clipper, often by the father. Parents stress preparing the child's feelings in advance.

### Cited Findings
- Order at one home ceremony: the хуурч played, said the ерөөл, then gave the first hair-cutter a bowl of milk with a хадаг (ceremonial silk scarf). That person tasted the milk and started cutting, and the fiddle played again in between. — [Group post, Aug 21 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2313831192749706/)
- A girl's ceremony at a camp (лагер): a relative gave a long, eloquent ерөөл to open. The mother shared her daughter's ивээл year, so she touched first. She regretted saying only "сайн хүн болоорой" ("be a good person") instead of a proper ерөөл. The father and an uncle then shaved the girl bald with a clipper on the spot, and latecomers "anointed" (мялаах) the bald head. — [Group post, Aug 1 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2295243264608499/)
- At a cafe, the order should have been the ивээл-year person first, then from the eldest down. Instead the venue's host (хөтлөгч) brought a whole table up at once, regardless of age. Tip: brief the host in advance. — [Group post, ~late Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2344646559668169/)
- Cutting in a separate room: guests queued, drank tea, said their ерөөл, gave a gift and took a photo. One hired ерөөлч first "cleansed the home's жавар (bad cold energy)" and then gave the blessing. — [Group post, Aug 15 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2308644699935022/); [Group post, Aug 21 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2313831192749706/)
- Preparing the child (seen in ~4 posts): tell the child for weeks ("we'll take your hair, you'll be bald"), show the clipper buzzing beforehand, and take the child to other ceremonies first. Children who were prepared sat calmly and did not cry. — [Group post, Aug 1 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2295243264608499/); [Group post, Aug 15 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2308644699935022/); [Group post, ~Aug–Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2319611175505041/)
- Group explainers describe the "traditional" steps:
  - Burn juniper (арц) and incense, and purify the scissors.
  - The ивээл person sits in the north-west (баруун хойно), the place of honor.
  - The host announces the start.
  - The ивээл person gives the child milk, anoints the forehead with milk, and first touches the hair with a **wooden knife** carved by the father. One version says to dip the knife in milk and drip the milk onto the scissors' tip.
  - **Boy: right temple first; girl: left temple first.**
  - Then cut with old, used scissors (never new ones), sometimes with a хадаг tied to them.
  - The child is carried sunwise (нар зөв) past the guests, who each snip a little.
  - The mother wraps the hair in a хадаг.
  - "Everyone who touches the hair tastes milk first."

  — [Group post, Jul 31 2025](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/1980054656127363/); [Group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2274331730032986/)
- The same girl-left / boy-right rule appears in a parent's comment. — [Group post, Jul 31 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2293742201425272/)
- If no ивээл-year person is among close family, "the father can touch first" (explainer). One family said "the father touches, only grandparents present". — [Group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2274331730032986/); [Group post, Aug 14 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2306690880130404/)
- Some explainers say the paternal and maternal uncles (авга, нагац) must be present. A parent asked if this is required, and the answer was "not mandatory". — [Group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2274331730032986/); [Group post, Oct 3 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2354060175393474/)
- A question that appeared but was not answered in what I loaded: "Must the first toucher be married? Can a 20-year-old cousin with the ивээл year do it?" — [Group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2306571063475719/)
- **Hygiene debate: the shared milk bowl** (Sept 11 2026, 70 comments). The majority (~6–7 comments) use small disposable cups (~30 g paper cups), one per person. One family put milk in a silver bowl and gave guests cheese cubes on toothpicks to dip. "Silver kills germs" was rejected by others as not true. One commenter wrote a long health warning about shared cups. Some give хорхой ааруул (a dried curd snack) instead of milk. — [Group post, Sept 11 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2332760014190157/); [Group post, Sept 25 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2346061212860037/)
- **Debate: big party vs. small family ritual** (Aug 14 2026, 44 comments). A lama told one mother:
  - Do it at home, not in a restaurant, so the merit (буян) stays.
  - Only parents, grandparents and siblings should touch the hair.
  - No layered boov plate and no ууц (sheep's rump, a festive meat dish); serve light white foods and cake.

  About 6 commenters agreed ("the lama is right"; "we avoid letting all sorts of people touch the child's head"). About 3–4 disagreed: "it's once, relatives aren't stones, nobody wishes the child harm", and "many guests should be treated even if only close ones touch". — [Group post, Aug 14 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2306690880130404/)
- Another explainer criticizes how the ritual "has become a mass party with coworkers", saying "you don't know who among guests is a thief or gossip". — [Group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2274331730032986/)

### Inferences
- In practice, the hired ерөөлч/хуурч now replaces the "most respected elder" role in many families. Hiring one was mentioned in ~9 posts.
- The order (ивээл person first, then by seniority) is the most common point of confusion at venues.

### Gaps
- I saw few first-hand descriptions of the wooden knife or sunwise carrying actually being done in 2026. These appear mainly in explainer posts.

## 4. What did they do with the hair?

### Takeaway
Almost everyone says to **keep all the hair**, including the clipper-shaved rest, in the сэвлэг pouch (уут), often wrapped in a хадаг. Some keep it in a framed keepsake. There are a few folk uses (as a remedy) and one countryside practice.

### Cited Findings
- In the Sept 2026 thread "Keep only the hair guests cut? What about the rest?" (32 comments), ~7 of 9 visible answers said put **all** the hair in the pouch. One reason given: "if a single strand ends up in a bird's nest, it's very bad". — [Group post, ~Sept 13 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2334040027395489/)
- Same thread: one mother left the shaved remainder for her two daughters, and noted that "nowadays barbers collect it for you". In the countryside some put the hair in the sheep pen "for many sheep", and keep it to use as a remedy (домнох) if the child gets sick. — [Group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2334040027395489/)
- Explainer: wrap the hair in a хадаг and keep it carefully. The smell of burnt сэвлэг hair is used to stop a child's nosebleed (a folk remedy, not checked). — [Group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2274331730032986/)
- Products: "сэвлэг хадгалах хүндэтгэлийн жааз" (a frame for keeping the hair, from the vendor Anikaa Wallart) was advertised in several threads. A parent chose the "Lily mom" set because its hair container stands on a holder. — [Group post, Aug 15 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2308644699935022/); [Group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2319611175505041/)
- A parent asked whether something (rice, juniper) should be put inside the pouch before cutting. The answers did not load. — [Group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2306571063475719/)

### Inferences
- "Keep all of it" is the clear community norm. Framed display of the hair is a growing commercial trend.

### Gaps
- No posts mentioned burying or burning the hair, or giving it to a temple.

## 5. What was on the table (ул боов layers, ууц or not, food, drinks)

### Takeaway
A typical 2026 table has:
- a modest **хэвийн боов** (moulded biscuit) tavag of **3 layers** (about 60,000₮),
- цагаан идээ (white dairy foods),
- a soup (often vegetable soup with mantuu buns) and a meat second course,
- 2–3 salads (нийслэл is almost always one),
- sliced fruit in small cups, a cake or cupcakes, and airag.

**Ууц is optional** and often skipped in the city. There is a strong consensus against **hard liquor**. Two food rules are hotly debated: closed dumplings (бууз/хуушуур) and whether ууц is needed.

### Cited Findings
- **Tavag (idee plate):**

  - 3 layers from "Өгөөмж Архад" ~60,000₮; "elders said a huge tavag isn't needed". — [Group post, Sept 19 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2339810083485150/)
  - "Round 5, up to 5 layers OK; we did 3 out of respect because two grandmothers were present". — [Group post, ~Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2342322679900557/)
  - One family made an all-ааруул idee from "Цагаа брэнд" for 178k. — [Group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2319611175505041/)
  - One family planned a big idee of ул боов plus a small idee of ааруул. — [Group post, Sept 25 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2346061212860037/)
- **Ууц:**

## 8. Blessing (ерөөл) texts people share

### Takeaway
People share **traditional ерөөл formulas** and ask for ready-made ones. The same three pieces repeat across posts:
- a scissors verse (opening golden and silver scissors "entrusts life" and "long life"),
- a "cut from the right temple, then the left, then the crown" verse comparing the cuts to a lotus blooming, knowledge radiating and wisdom sharpening,
- a short wish formula for long life and helping one's parents.

Vendors also post new rhymed 4-line verses for boys and girls. Not reproduced in full here, because of copyright caution; please read them at the links.

### Cited Findings
- A business page (Хүүхдийн бэлэг Giftbox kids, ~Oct 2026) posted the "right temple, left temple, crown" verse. It also posted 3 four-line verses "for a boy" (themes: a good deeds master, shining like moonlight, manly dignity, strong like a mountain, support for the homeland) and 3 "for a girl" (themes: kind-hearted and wise, light of the world, support for mother and father, shining like the moon, harmony). The verses use alliterative openings such as "Сэвлэгийг чинь санан үргээж". A group member linked it as "ерөөл if you need one". — [Facebook page post](https://www.facebook.com/story.php?story_fbid=1403039485158965&id=100063587802752); [link shared in group, Oct 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2354060175393474/)

## 11. Common tips, mistakes and complaints

### Takeaway
The most repeated lessons are:
- Do not over-order food; guests eat little and many invited people do not come.
- Hire or assign helpers and a photographer.
- Book venues and the ерөөлч early, and send invitations a month ahead.
- Brief the host and ерөөлч on the order and the number of tunes.
- Prepare the child's feelings.
- Do not exhaust yourself; parents want time to sit and enjoy the day.

### Inferences
- The strongest unmet needs are: a realistic guest-count and food calculator; a checklist with lead times (venue 1.5–2 months, invitations 1 month, deel early); a run-sheet for the host or ерөөлч (cutting order, number of tunes); and a budget template. The posts with full budgets got the most engagement (748 and 330 reactions).

### Gaps
- Few posts mention alcohol-related problems at ceremonies. The alcohol talk is about rules, not incidents.
- Comment threads were only partly loaded, so complaint frequencies are underestimated.
