# Host a Mongolian first haircut with confidence

**In short:** The core is simple, and almost all sources share it. On a good morning chosen with a lama, a respected person cuts the child's first lock with scissors tied to a хадаг (khadag, a ceremonial silk scarf). The child tastes milk. Then every guest cuts a little hair, says a blessing (ерөөл) and gives a gift. The hair is kept, never thrown away. The age, the day, the food rules and the size of the party change by family, region and lama, so your elders have the last word. Parents report that a home party for 30–50 guests cost about 2.5–3.5 million ₮ in 2026. A café cost about twice as much.

You can host this ceremony well. Follow a small, stable core, and let your own family decide the rest. The custom has many names: үсний найр ("hair feast"), сэвлэг үргээх ёслол and даахь үргээх (both mean "taking the first hair"). It is on Mongolia's **National Register of Intangible Cultural Heritage** ([IRCI / NCCH record](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13419)). Sources agree on the main steps. They do not agree on the details: the child's exact age, who cuts first, which day is lucky, which foods are allowed, and how many guests to invite. Different lamas often give different dates. Different regions put different people first: for example, the eldest relative in Khalkh sources, but the mother's brother among Kalmyks and some Buryats. Many regional customs are not written anywhere online, so your family's memory matters most for those. This guide gives you a safe "most common" plan, a run sheet for the day, a timeline, prices, blessing texts, a table of regional versions, a list of disagreements, and questions to ask your elders.

## Many websites copy one text, so check how strong each source is

There is no official rulebook. This guide comes from five research files. They cover Mongolian websites and books, two academic papers, the national heritage record, foreign visitors' blogs, and a Facebook parents' group. These sources are not equally strong.

Many Mongolian news sites copy the same article. So "five websites say it" can still mean one source. The researchers found three main copied texts. **Text family A** is the book by Х.Сампилдэндэв, Н.Уртнасан and Т.Дорждагва, *Монгол зан үйл, баяр ёслолын товчоон* (2006); 24tsag.mn and the medeelelzui blog copy it. **Text family B** is the big customs dictionaries by Ч.Арьяасүрэн & Х.Нямбуу and by Г.Чингэл; ugiinbichig.mn and the namurana blog copy them. **Text family C** is one news text of unknown origin; updown.mn, zindaa.mn and huree.mn copy it. More independent voices are: Д.Цогтбаатар, a lama and astrologer at Gandan monastery, interviewed by ikon.mn in 2016; the record of the National Center for Cultural Heritage; a 2024 study that watched eight ceremonies (*Nomadic Studies*); a 2025 paper (*JECS*); and blogs by foreign guests. The 2025 paper has clear translation errors (it calls the hair "the ear"), so this guide treats its history claims as weak.

**Points from Facebook are labelled [FB: personal experience].** They come from about 27 posts read in full in the public group ["Сэвлэг үргээх ёсон"](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/) (about 101,000 members), mostly from August–October 2026, plus a few business pages. They show what parents really do and think. They are personal opinion from community posts, not authority. Counts like "in about 6 posts" are only what the researcher could load. They are not a survey. No private names are given.

**Prices** are approximate. Each price shows its source and year. Prices change quickly, so always call the vendor.

## Order of the day: from purifying the scissors to the last snip

Traditional sources say to start **in the morning, before noon** ([ugiinbichig.mn](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi); [zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)). Guests who arrive after noon traditionally do not touch the child's hair ([updown.mn, 2018](https://updown.mn/50592.html)). The steps below combine the traditional sources with 2026 practice. The clock times are only an example for a 10:00 start; no source gives exact times. One hotel ceremony in Ulaanbaatar lasted about 3 hours ([foreign guest's blog, 2012](https://livefromub.wordpress.com/2012/05/17/a-mongolian-hair-cutting-ceremony/)).

**Before the guests arrive (about 8:00–9:30)**

1. Set the table. Put the цагаалга, a cup of fresh milk, small cups, airag and the тавгийн идээ on it. Cook food fresh on the day; never serve leftovers ([arkhangai.mn, 2018](http://arkhangai.mn/index.php?newsid=3616)).
2. Tie the хадаг to the scissors handle. Burn juniper (арц) or incense (хүж) and pass the scissors through the smoke to purify them ([zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); [Huree.mn, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/)).
3. Families who keep the old way also make an incense offering (сан тавих) and sprinkle the first of the tea or milk (цацал) to the land ([updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html)). One Facebook explainer says to do this on a hill **[FB: personal experience]** ([group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2274331730032986/)).
4. Give the MC and the ерөөлч a written plan: who cuts first, the cutting order, and how many tunes to play. **[FB: personal experience]** One family's café host called a whole table up at once, ignoring age order, so brief the host in advance ([group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2344646559668169/)).

**Guests arrive (about 9:30–10:00)**

5. Dress the child well. Seat the child at the head of the table. Seat the first cutter in the honoured place: north-west (баруун хойно) in a ger, or at the head table in a hall ([Huree.mn, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/); [IRCI](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13419)).

**Opening (about 10:00)**

6. The feast leader (the father, a grandfather or the MC) welcomes everyone. Airag is offered first (айраг сөн барих), and a blessing is said ([arkhangai.mn, 2018](http://arkhangai.mn/index.php?newsid=3616)). **[FB: personal experience]** In one home, the fiddle player played, said the blessing, then gave the first cutter a bowl of milk with a хадаг ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2313831192749706/)).
7. Some families let guests taste the цагаалга now, to open the feast. Others serve it after the cutting. Both orders appear in good sources (see "Where sources disagree").

**The core ritual (about 10:15–11:15)**

8. The leader announces: **"За одоо хүүгийнхээ (охиныхоо) даахийг үргээе"** ("Now let us take our son's (daughter's) first hair"). The child stands up ([ugiinbichig.mn](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi); [medeelelzui](https://medeelelzui.blogmn.net/44325/huuhdiin-daahi-urgeeh-yos.html)).
9. The first cutter touches the child's hair with the **wooden knife**. In one version, the knife is dipped in milk and a drop falls on the scissors, "so the blade does not harm the child" ([Borderless Journal, 2025](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/)).
10. With the хадаг-tied scissors, the first cutter cuts a small lock: **right temple for a boy, left temple for a girl**. The lock is tied into the хадаг ([ikon.mn, 2016](https://ikon.mn/n/o1f); [namurana, citing Чингэл](http://namurana.blogspot.com/2011/08/blog-post_6221.html)).
11. The child tastes milk from a cup, and milk is dabbed on the child's forehead. The first cutter says a blessing and gives a gift ([24tsag.mn, 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553)). (Some families do the milk before the first cut.)
12. The child is led **sunwise** (clockwise) around the room. Parents, grandparents and relatives cut next, from the oldest down, then the other guests ([mongolmom blog, 2016](https://mongolmom.blogspot.com/2016/04/mongolian-childrens-first-hair-cutting.html); [updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html)). Each person sips milk from their own small cup, cuts a small lock, says a short blessing, and puts a gift in the pouch.
13. The mother gathers all the hair into the хадаг. If an important relative cannot come, leave one lock uncut until they arrive ([24tsag.mn, 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553); [IRCI](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13419)).

**Blessing and feast (about 11:15–13:00)**

14. A long formal blessing by the ерөөлч or an elder, with music. (Some families do this at the opening instead.)
15. Serve цагаалга to everyone and a bowl to each child, then the meal ([updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html); [zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)). Songs follow. In the countryside, some families add a small naadam (games) with wrestling and horse races ([24tsag.mn, 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755)).

**Finish**

16. Shave or trim the rest of the hair with a clipper. **[FB: personal experience]** Some do it on the spot, often the father ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2295243264608499/)). Others go to a barber later; one mother noted that "nowadays barbers collect the hair for you" ([group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2334040027395489/)).
17. Give your thank-you gifts: to the first cutter, to the children, and white food or milk to everyone who touched the hair. Thank your guests.

## Start planning two to three months ahead

No Mongolian planner or parenting site publishes a full checklist. This timeline combines tips from the sources with lessons that parents shared on Facebook (marked).

**2–3 months before**

- [ ] Ask your elders the questions in the last section of this guide.
- [ ] Work out the child's хий нас and the right year.
- [ ] Visit your family's lama or astrologer. Ask for: the date, the time window, and the child's ивээл years (to choose the first cutter).
- [ ] Choose the first cutter and ask them early.
- [ ] Decide: home or venue. Make a first guest list and budget.

**About 2 months before**

- [ ] Book the venue. **[FB: personal experience]** One family found that all the cafés they wanted were full 1.5 months before ([group post, 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2319611175505041/)).
- [ ] Book the ерөөлч and морин хуурч for a morning slot. Ask how long the blessing is and how many tunes they will play **[FB: personal experience]** ([group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2339810083485150/)).
- [ ] Book a photographer, or give one relative the job. **[FB]** Several hosts had "almost no photos" ([group post, Oct 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2358849451581213/)).
- [ ] If at home: book a caterer or cooks; rent tables, chairs and pots.
- [ ] Order the child's deel (traditional robe) or Mongol shirt. **[FB]** Popular makers are busy, so order early ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2308644699935022/)).

**1 month before**

- [ ] Send invitations. **[FB]** "Sending invitations 4 days before was a big mistake; send a month ahead" ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2295243264608499/)). Many parents make them on Canva and send them to family group chats ([group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2339810083485150/)).
- [ ] Ask each guest to confirm clearly whether they will come.
- [ ] Order the hair set (pouch, хадаг, scissors), a wooden knife (or the father carves it), a clipper, and a frame if you want one.
- [ ] Order the тавгийн идээ and the airag. Plan the menu.
- [ ] Order gift bags for children (about 10 more than expected) and a thank-you gift for the first cutter.
- [ ] Write a one-page run sheet for the MC and ерөөлч.

**1–2 weeks before**

- [ ] Count guests again and adjust the food. **[FB]** "We planned 50, 35 came; food was left over" ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2313831192749706/)).
- [ ] Order the cake. Bakeries need 2–3 days or more, and fill up early in busy times ([eguur.mn, 2022](https://eguur.mn/387675/)).
- [ ] Prepare the child. **[FB]** Tell the child for weeks ("we'll take your hair"), let them hear the clipper buzz, and take them to another ceremony first. Prepared children sat calmly ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2308644699935022/)).
- [ ] Give jobs to helpers: tea, dishes, photos, children's gifts, the gift pouch. **[FB]** "A home party is like Tsagaan Sar: one dishwasher, one for tea, two cooks" ([group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2336733743792784/)).
- [ ] Write a few lines of blessing for the parents and the first cutter.

**The day before**

- [ ] Tie the хадаг to the scissors. Charge the clipper. Prepare milk, small cups, juniper, the wooden knife and the pouch.
- [ ] Buy fresh fruit and milk.

**On the day**

- [ ] Cook the цагаалга fresh. Start before noon. Follow the order of the day above.

**After the day**

- [ ] Store the hair safely. Thank the first cutter again.
- [ ] Write down what you did. Many families use the same age and customs for all their children ([zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)), so your notes help with the next child.

## The rite turns a baby into a child

**The names.** Сэвлэг and даахь both mean a small child's first hair, never cut since birth. The national dictionary defines "сэвлэг үргээх" as "хүүхдийн үс авах" (to take a child's hair). It also lists "нялх хүүхдийн онгон үс" (a baby's untouched hair) as one meaning of сэвлэг ([mongoltoli.mn](https://mongoltoli.mn/search.php?opt=1&ug_id=81271&word=%D0%A1%D0%AD%D0%92%D0%9B%D0%AD%D0%93)). The word сэвлэг already appears in the 13th-century *Secret History of the Mongols* ([mongoltoli.mn](https://mongoltoli.mn/search.php?opt=1&ug_id=81271&word=%D0%A1%D0%AD%D0%92%D0%9B%D0%AD%D0%93)). This shows that the word is old; it does not prove the rite's age. Other names are даахь авах, даахь үргээх, өрөвлөг авах and үсний найр ([24tsag.mn, 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755); [ugiinbichig.mn](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi)). **[FB: personal experience]** A group explainer lists more local names, such as үс бандгайлах, өрвөг хайчлах, симиг хайчлах and алтан хонхолцог хайчлах, but does not say which group uses which ([group post, Jul 2025](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/1980054656127363/)).

**The meaning.** The rite marks the step from baby to child. It is held when the child can feel that the party is for them. That is why it is a bigger event than угаалга, the washing rite for a newborn ([ugiinbichig.mn](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi); [namurana](http://namurana.blogspot.com/2011/08/blog-post_6221.html)). Guests wish the child long life, lasting happiness, kindness to the parents, service to the country, and respect among people ([ikon.mn, 2022](https://ikon.mn/n/2gs3); [IRCI](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13419)). People also believe the haircut removes bad things. One widely copied text says that if the first hair is not taken, the child "will be angry and not clever" (ууртай, ухаан муутай болно) ([updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html)). A 2024 study watched eight ceremonies and interviewed nine people. Parents hoped the haircut would "block misfortunes" and heal a bad temper, an illness or slow development ([Nomadic Studies, 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25)).

**The old hairstyles had meanings too.** Traditionally, a boy kept one tuft (тэвэг), a sign that he would become a "төрийн хар хүн" (a lay man who serves the state). A girl kept two tufts (хөхөл), a sign that she would become a "гэрийн эх" (the mother of a household) ([24tsag.mn, 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553)).

**History: very old, but poorly documented.** Mongolian sources only say the custom comes "from ancient times" (эрт дээр үеэс) and give no dated record ([24tsag.mn, 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553); [ikon.mn, 2022](https://ikon.mn/n/2gs3)). The rite is clearly mixed with Buddhist astrology: lamas advise on dates and years ([foreign visitor's blog, 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html); [ikon.mn, 2016](https://ikon.mn/n/o1f)). A child who will become a lama has all of the hair cut, with no tufts left ([ugiinbichig.mn, citing Арьяасүрэн & Нямбуу](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi)). A 2025 paper claims shamanic roots, use by Chinggis Khaan "to promote unity", and Buddhist additions in the 16th century ([JECS, 2025](https://www.jecs.pl/index.php/jecs/article/view/1790)). Its sources are weak and its text has errors, so treat these claims as unproven. A Chinese scholar writes that the blessing poems of the Deed Mongols (Oirat descendants in Qinghai) "evolved from shaman sacrificial words" ([ihchina.cn, 2023](https://www.ihchina.cn/luntan_details/27930.html)). A foreign resident wrote in 2012 that the ceremony had a "resurgence" after 1991 ([foreign guest's blog, 2012](https://livefromub.wordpress.com/2012/05/17/a-mongolian-hair-cutting-ceremony/)). The safe summary: the custom is old, and it mixes folk beliefs with Buddhist astrology.

## Boys at odd ages, girls at even ages, counted the Mongolian way

**The main rule** in most sources: **boys at 3 or 5, girls at 2 or 4** ([IRCI](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13419); [ugiinbichig.mn](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi); [medeelelzui](https://medeelelzui.blogmn.net/44325/huuhdiin-daahi-urgeeh-yos.html); [zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); [Borderless Journal, 2025](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/)). The reason is number symbolism. Odd numbers stand for арга, the male principle ("method" or action). Even numbers stand for билиг, the female principle ("wisdom") ([ikon.mn, 2022](https://ikon.mn/n/2gs3); [24tsag.mn, 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755)).

**The age is хий нас, not the birthday age.** In February 2016, the Gandan lama-astrologer Д.Цогтбаатар gave these examples: a girl born in 2015 (Sheep year) is "2"; a boy born in 2014 (Horse) is "3"; a girl born in 2013 (Snake) is "4"; a boy born in 2012 (Dragon) is "5" ([ikon.mn, 2016](https://ikon.mn/n/o1f)). These numbers only work in хий нас: the child is 1 at birth and gains a year at each Tsagaan Sar. A simple example: a boy born in spring 2023 (after Tsagaan Sar) becomes "3" at Tsagaan Sar 2025. So he could have his ceremony in 2025, when he is only about 2 by birthday count.

**Be careful: people count хий нас in two ways.** Some sources describe it as "1 at birth, plus one year at every Tsagaan Sar". Others describe it as "the real age plus one year for the time in the womb" ([FB group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2237105633755596/)). For most children both give the same answer. For a child born just before Tsagaan Sar, they can differ by a year. So tell the lama the exact birth date and ask: "What is my child's хий нас now?"

**[FB: personal experience] Much of the online fighting is really about counting.** In one thread, a parent wrote "a girl at real age 3, which is хий нас 4", and called it "girls at an odd age". Others replied "girls even by хий нас, boys odd by хий нас" ([group post, ~June 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2237105633755596/)). Always say which count you mean.

**Other versions exist:**

- An old oral line says: "Охин хүүхдийн даахийг хөхөөн дуунаар 2, 4, 6 насанд, бүстэй хүүхдийн даахийг бугын дуунаар 3, 5, 7 насанд нь үргээ" ("Take a girl's hair at 2, 4 or 6 with the cuckoo's call; a boy's (literally 'a child with a belt') at 3, 5 or 7 with the stag's call") ([updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html)).
- One text gives girls 4 or 6, and a delicate or much-loved child 7 ([TANiiNOM text via pdfcoffee](https://pdfcoffee.com/--14601-pdf-free.html)).
- In the past, girls' hair was sometimes only lightly touched at 7 or 9 ([zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)).
- One study gives boys 1, 3 or 5, and says Dörvöd families cut at 3 or 5 for both boys and girls ([Nomadic Studies, 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25)).
- **[FB: personal experience]** A long group post cites a lecture by folk researcher С.Дамбий. It says both boys and girls should be cut at an odd age, mainly 5, when the child can say "чулуу" ("stone") clearly, in an ивээл year. It calls "girls at an even age" a mistake spread over the last ~50 years, and says even ages were used only quietly for sickly children ([group post, ~mid-2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2274331730032986/)).
- **[FB: personal experience]** One commenter said western aimags (provinces) use the reverse rule: girls odd, boys even ([group post, Jul 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2293742201425272/)). Another said some areas do it at the first birthday ([group post, Jul 2025](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/1980054656127363/)).
- One US visitor's blog says "boys in even years, girls in odd years" ([blog, 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html)). Every Mongolian source says the opposite, so treat this as an error.

**Brothers and sisters.** Many families use the same age for all their children as they used for the first-born ([zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)). Some cut all the children's hair at once when the first-born's is cut ([Nomadic Studies, 2024, citing Sampildendev 2006](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25)). No survey shows which age or which count most city families use today.

## A lama usually picks a waxing-moon morning

**Who decides.** Lamas traditionally advise on the day and the year ([foreign visitor's blog, 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html); [Nomadic Studies, 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25)). **[FB: personal experience]** Most parents still ask a lama. They give the child's birth-year animal, and sometimes the birth month and day ([group post, Oct 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2349832365816255/)).

**Which month.** Д.Цогтбаатар says: "Хүүхдийн даахийг сар шинээр эсвэл ивээл сард нь авдаг" (the hair is taken in the Lunar New Year month or in the child's ивээл month). He adds that Mongolians try to fit such events into **lunar days 1–15**, because the moon grows during those days ([ikon.mn, 2016](https://ikon.mn/n/o1f)).

**The friendly (ивээл) years.** The 12 animals form four friendly groups of three ([ugluu.mn, search snippet](https://ugluu.mn/219916.html); [mongoltoli.mn, "ивээл жил"](https://mongoltoli.mn/dictionary/detail/51241)):

- Tiger – Dog – Horse (Бар – Нохой – Морь)
- Mouse – Dragon – Monkey (Хулгана – Луу – Бич)
- Ox – Snake – Rooster (Үхэр – Могой – Тахиа)
- Pig – Sheep – Rabbit (Гахай – Хонь – Туулай)

For example, a Rabbit-year child's friendly animals are the Pig and the Sheep. The heritage record also mentions a person "born in the same zodiac year" sitting behind the child ([IRCI](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13419)). The child's ивээл months seem to be the lunar months that carry the same animals. Цогтбаатар's examples fit this: for a Horse-year boy he named the first spring month (Tiger), the middle summer month (Horse) and the last autumn month (Dog) ([ikon.mn, 2016](https://ikon.mn/n/o1f)). This pattern is the researchers' reading, so confirm it with an astrologer.

**Season.** Traditionally a girl's hair is cut in mid-summer, "when the cuckoo calls", and a boy's in mid-autumn, "when the stag calls" (буга урамдах) ([Borderless Journal, 2025](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/); [IRCI](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13419)). One text gives exact days: a girl's on the 8th day of the middle summer month, a boy's on the 15th day of the middle autumn month, "even better on a Monday or Friday" ([updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html); [24tsag.mn, 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553)). Avoid the cold. One study says it is "not preferred" before the end of spring or after autumn, because the child may catch a cold or a "head sickness" ([Nomadic Studies, 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25)). **[FB: personal experience]** Some parents say girls should be done in spring, and note that many girls are now done in autumn anyway ([group post, Jul 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2293742201425272/)).

**Good lunar days and what they promise.** Two Mongolian pages (probably one shared text) give this list ([ikon.mn, 2022 (advertorial)](https://ikon.mn/n/2gs3); [Huree.mn, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/)):

| Lunar day | Promised benefit | Lunar day | Promised benefit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3rd | wealth and livestock | 14th | more livestock |
| 4th | a strong body | 15th | good fortune |
| 5th | more livestock | 19th | good friends |
| 6th | a healthy look | 22nd | plenty of food and drink |
| 8th | long life | 23rd | property |
| 10th | energy | 26th | lasting joy |
| 11th | a sharp mind | 27th | good fortune |
| 13th | joy | | |

Both pages say to check the weekday too, not only the lunar day. Note that this list includes days after the 15th, which does not match the "days 1–15 only" advice. Ask your lama. One article also says a day is very good when "Балжинням" and "Дашням" fall together ([24tsag.mn, 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755)); no source explains these astrological terms, so ask your lama about them too.

**Weekdays.** **Thursday (Пүрэв) is avoided**, because it is "хүний хишиг өдөр" (a day that belongs to people's good fortune) ([24tsag.mn, 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553); [24tsag.mn, 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755)). The folklorist С.Дулам gave an interview titled "Хүүхдийн даахийг пүрэв гарагт авдаггүй байсан" ("The child's hair was not taken on Thursday") ([tusgal.mn](http://tusgal.mn/s-dulam-khuukhdiin-daakhiig-purew-garagt-awdaggui-baisan); the page itself would not load). A copied text says "some areas" avoid "the fourth day" ([updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html)). This almost certainly means Thursday, the fourth day of the week. Monday and Friday are the best days ([updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html); [24tsag.mn, 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553)). **[FB: personal experience]** Group explainers add Wednesday as good, and list good "animal days": mouse, ox, sheep, monkey, dog and pig ([group post, Jul 2025](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/1980054656127363/)).

**Time of day.** In the morning, before noon, "while the sun is young" (наран залуу үед) ([ugiinbichig.mn](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi); [updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html)). Цогтбаатар gave one child the window 11:40–13:40 ([ikon.mn, 2016](https://ikon.mn/n/o1f)). **[FB: personal experience]** Two families were each given 9:40–11:40 ([group post, Oct 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2349832365816255/)), and ерөөлч bookings run about 9:30–13:30 ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2290836038382555/)).

**[FB: personal experience] Different lamas give different dates.** In one October 2026 thread with 30 comments, Tiger-year boys got October 13, 14, 15, 18 or 21 from different lamas. One lama said a Dragon-year boy must do it on October 4, because "after the 11th the month is харш" ([group post, Oct 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2349832365816255/)). Parents also warned that a day can be an ивээл day and a "хар нохой" (black dog, a bad) day at the same time, and that a "repeated" lunar day (a date that appears twice in the lunar month) should be avoided ([same post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2349832365816255/); [group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2334234204042738/)). One couple picked a date from a Google "good haircut day" calendar, and it later matched the child's ивээл month and day ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2306690880130404/)). One explainer says, "Grandparents say the child chooses the day itself" ([group post, Jul 2025](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/1980054656127363/)).

**Modern change.** Lucky days often fall on workdays. So many city families now hold it on a weekend ([JECS, 2025](https://www.jecs.pl/index.php/jecs/article/view/1790); [Nomadic Studies, 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25)). One 2014 family held it during the Tsagaan Sar holidays ([blog, 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html)). **Simple advice:** choose one lama, ask why the day is good, and if you must move to a weekend, ask the lama to pick the best weekend day.

## The first cutter is the most honoured guest

| Role | Who usually does it | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| **First cutter** | A person born in one of the child's ивээл years. Д.Цогтбаатар says it should be a man ([ikon.mn, 2016](https://ikon.mn/n/o1f)). Older ethnography says the grandfather, father or eldest respected guest among Khalkh, Dörvöd, Bayad, Myangad and Khotgoid ([24tsag.mn, 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755)). | Sits in the honoured seat. Touches the hair first, cuts the first lock, gives milk, blesses, gives a gift. Becomes "the most honoured person of this family" that day ([Huree.mn, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/)). |
| **Feast leader** (найрын ахлагч) | The father, a grandfather, the host; today often a hired MC (хөтлөгч) | Opens the feast and announces the haircut ([zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)) |
| **Mother** | The child's mother | Gathers the hair into the хадаг ([24tsag.mn, 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553)). Traditionally sews the child's deel ([Borderless Journal, 2025](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/)). |
| **Father** | The child's father | Traditionally carves the wooden knife ([Borderless Journal, 2025](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/)). **[FB]** Often shaves the head at the end. |
| **Uncles and aunts** (авга, нагац) | Father's-side and mother's-side relatives | One text says they "must be present" ([updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html)). The eldest maternal relative "plays a significant role" ([Nomadic Studies, 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25)). |
| **Ерөөлч and хуурч** | Hired reciter and fiddle player, or an eloquent elder | Open with music and a long blessing. **[FB]** Mentioned in about 9 posts; they now often take the elder's blessing role. |
| **Lama** | The family's lama or astrologer | Chooses the date and time. Only one 2025 paper has a monk reciting at the event ([JECS, 2025](https://www.jecs.pl/index.php/jecs/article/view/1790)); no Mongolian source does. |
| **Helpers** | Relatives or hired staff | Tea, dishes, serving, photos, children's gifts ([FB group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2339810083485150/)) |

**What kind of person should cut first?** Sources ask for a person with a good, stable life, because "the child will follow a similar successful life path" ([Nomadic Studies, 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25)). One dictionary says to give the scissors to "an elder with many children" ([namurana, citing Чингэл](http://namurana.blogspot.com/2011/08/blog-post_6221.html)). A personal essay says people who touch the hair "should be virtuous, fortunate, and stable" ([Borderless Journal, 2025](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/)). In practice it can be young: in a 2014 home ceremony, "a young cousin whose lunar birth year matched the child's cut first", and an older neighbour who knew the customs cut second ([blog, 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html)). **[FB: personal experience]** If no ивээл person is among close family, "the father can touch first" ([group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2274331730032986/)). One parent asked whether the first cutter must be married, or whether a 20-year-old cousin could do it; no answer loaded ([group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2306571063475719/)). **[FB]** When asked whether the uncles must come, one answer was "not mandatory" ([group post, Oct 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2354060175393474/)).

## Each ritual step has a small meaning and many local versions

### Getting the tools ready

The scissors have a **хадаг tied to the handle** ([Huree.mn, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/)). Foreign guests describe it as **blue** ([blog, 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html); [blog, 2014](https://bransnmongolia.blogspot.com/2014/10/hair-cutting-ceremony.html); [blog, 2012](https://livefromub.wordpress.com/2012/05/17/a-mongolian-hair-cutting-ceremony/)), and one Mongolian dictionary text names a blue хадаг too ([namurana](http://namurana.blogspot.com/2011/08/blog-post_6221.html)). In one home, the hair was knotted into one end of the хадаг and the scissors were tied to the other end ([blog, 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html)). The scissors are purified in juniper or incense smoke ([zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)). **[FB: personal experience]** One explainer says to use old, used scissors, never new ones ([group post, Jul 2025](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/1980054656127363/)); no other source mentions this.

The **wooden knife** makes the first touch gentle. The father traditionally carves it ([Borderless Journal, 2025](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/)). One text calls the touch "домноох", a folk charm ([updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html)). One blog says the knife is also tied with a хадаг ([mongolmom, 2016](https://mongolmom.blogspot.com/2016/04/mongolian-childrens-first-hair-cutting.html)). One copied text says the wooden knife is used only "in some regions" ([Huree.mn, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/)). City families often skip it.

### First touch and first cut

Almost every source agrees: **the first cutter touches the hair with the wooden knife, then cuts a lock with the хадаг-tied scissors** ([24tsag.mn, 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553); [ugiinbichig.mn](https://www.ugiinbichig.mn/AboutDaahi); [IRCI](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13419)). The side is clear in many sources: "хүү бол баруун, охин бол зүүн шанаанаас" — from the **right temple for a boy, the left temple for a girl** ([updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html); [ikon.mn, 2016](https://ikon.mn/n/o1f); [namurana](http://namurana.blogspot.com/2011/08/blog-post_6221.html)). One copied text instead says to start on the right side of the crown, then the forehead ([zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)). The long blessing names the right temple, the left temple, the crown and the back of the head, in that order (see the appendix).

### Milk

The child tastes milk from a cup, and milk is dabbed on the forehead ([24tsag.mn, 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553); [mongolmom, 2016](https://mongolmom.blogspot.com/2016/04/mongolian-childrens-first-hair-cutting.html)). Most sources put this after the first cut; the heritage record puts it before the cutting ([IRCI](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13419)). In one study, "everyone at the ceremony sips milk" ([Nomadic Studies, 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25)). In a 2014 home, each cutter was given milk tea or airag, then offered it to the child; milk was also rubbed into the hair before each cut ([blog, 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html)).

**[FB: personal experience] The shared milk bowl is a hygiene worry.** In a thread with 70 comments, most families used small disposable paper cups (about 30 g), one per person. One family put milk in a silver bowl and gave guests cheese cubes on toothpicks to dip. Others rejected the idea that "silver kills germs". Some families give хорхой ааруул (a small dried-curd snack) instead of milk ([group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2332760014190157/)).

### Guests cut in turn

The child is led **sunwise** past parents, elders and guests. Each person cuts a little hair, adds it to the хадаг, says a blessing and gives a gift ([updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html); [24tsag.mn, 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553)). One blog says "in order of age, moving in the direction of the sun" ([mongolmom, 2016](https://mongolmom.blogspot.com/2016/04/mongolian-childrens-first-hair-cutting.html)). One paper says parents first, then grandparents, then others ([JECS, 2025](https://www.jecs.pl/index.php/jecs/article/view/1790)). In one 2014 home, after the first two cutters, people "cut in no apparent order" ([blog, 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html)). **[FB: personal experience]** Some families do the cutting in a separate room: guests queue, drink tea, say their blessing, give a gift and take a photo ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2308644699935022/)). A tired child may refuse the milk near the end ([blog, 2014](https://mininmongolia.wordpress.com/2014/08/12/the-hair-cutting-ceremony-and-rug-washing-day/)).

### The final shave and the tufts

The heritage record says that after everyone has touched the hair, the head is shaved. Boys keep locks above the forehead (тэвэг); girls keep locks on both sides ([IRCI](https://www.irci.jp/rdb/item/13419)). Other names: a boy's single tuft is "ганц тав"; a girl's two small tufts at the temples are "хөхөл санчиг" ([namurana](http://namurana.blogspot.com/2011/08/blog-post_6221.html)). In the western aimags, a girl's tufts are called **сонжуу** and a boy's three-finger-wide strip on the forehead is called **согсоо**. The general word for these uncut patches is **эвэр** ("horn"). The same text says "Одоо цагт энэ ёс бараг орхигджээ" (today this custom is almost abandoned) ([zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); [updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html)).

Today, girls are often only trimmed, not shaved ([medeelelzui](https://medeelelzui.blogmn.net/44325/huuhdiin-daahi-urgeeh-yos.html)). Some families shave with an electric clipper after the symbolic cuts ([Mongolian Store](https://mongolianstore.com/tradition-of-first-hair-cutting-ceremony/)). **[FB: personal experience]** At one girl's ceremony, the father and an uncle shaved her head with a clipper on the spot, and latecomers "blessed" (мялаах) the bald head ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2295243264608499/)). No source says who should do the final shave.

After the rite, longer hair should be braided and tied with camel-wool thread, a leather strap (сур) or a tassel (цацаг). Loose, tangled hair is a sign of mourning and is believed to shorten life ([updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html); [Huree.mn, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/)).

## The cut hair is kept for life, never thrown away

**Keep it.** The mother wraps the hair in the хадаг and keeps it ("эх нь сэвийг нь авч хадганд боодог") ([24tsag.mn, 2017](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/155553); [zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)). The first hair is "not thrown away because it is considered sacred" ([Nomadic Studies, 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25)).

**It is a folk remedy.** If the child falls ill, a little hair can be used. When the child is sick, family members "smoke out of the first hair to expel the evil and sickness" ([Nomadic Studies, 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25)). One article says burnt hair is smelled to stop a nosebleed ([updown.mn, 2018](https://updown.mn/50592.html)). Burning a few hairs is a healing use; it is not a way to get rid of the bundle.

**Never throw it away.** "Үс гэзэгний сэвээ хамаагүй хаяхыг онцгой цээрлэдэг" (throwing away hair carelessly is strictly avoided). It is believed to damage merit and call bad luck. Birds such as magpies use hair left outside for their nests, and this "brings suffering" ([updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html)).

**[FB: personal experience] Keep all of it.** In a thread asking "keep only the hair guests cut, or the rest too?", about 7 of 9 answers said put **all** the hair, including the clipper-shaved rest, in the pouch. One reason: "if a single strand ends up in a bird's nest, it's very bad". In the countryside, some families put the shaved hair in the sheep pen "for many sheep" ([group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2334040027395489/)). Framed keepsakes ("сэвлэг хадгалах хүндэтгэлийн жааз") are now sold, for example by Anikaa Wallart; one parent chose a "Lily mom" set because the hair container stands on a holder ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2308644699935022/); [group post, 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2319611175505041/)).

**Giving it back later.** One family keeps the хадаг and gives it to the child in the late pre-teen or early teen years ([blog, 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html)). One study says a small piece is kept with a coin and өрөм (dried milk cream) until age 12, when the child "is considered an adult"; the region is unclear ([Nomadic Studies, 2024](https://nomadicstudies.org/journal/article/view/25)).

No Mongolian source tells families to bury or burn the whole bundle. Other peoples do it differently (see the regional section): some Buryats once put the hair in a tree for birds, which is the opposite of the Khalkh taboo.

## Цагаалга is the one dish every source requires

**Required: цагаалга.** "Энэ найранд заавал байх ёстой идээний зүйл нь цагаалга юм" (the food that must be at this feast is цагаалга) ([updown.mn, 2018](https://updown.mn/55761.html); [zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)). It is white rice cooked in milk, with аарц (soft dried curd) and raisins: "үзэмтэй аарцтай, сүүтэй будаа" ([arkhangai.mn, 2018](http://arkhangai.mn/index.php?newsid=3616); [Huree.mn, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/)). Every guest tastes it, and **every visiting child gets a bowl** ([zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)). **[FB: personal experience]** No first-hand 2026 post mentioned serving цагаалга, only the explainer posts. It may be getting forgotten, so put it on your list.

**Also needed:** a cup of fresh milk for the ritual, and fresh food. Hosts serve "аль болох шинэ соргог идээ ундаа" (food and drink as fresh as possible) and never old or leftover food ([arkhangai.mn, 2018](http://arkhangai.mn/index.php?newsid=3616)).

**Expected:** airag for the first toast, and white dairy foods (ааруул, cheese, butter, cream).

**Optional, by family choice:** a тавгийн идээ; meat dishes; битүүлэг (a "closed" dish), which depends on local custom ([Huree.mn table article, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D0%B4%D0%B0%D0%B0%D1%85%D1%8C-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D0%BD%D0%B0%D0%B9%D1%80%D1%8B%D0%BD-%D1%88%D0%B8%D1%80%D1%8D%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D1%85/)); sweets for the children ([Huree.mn food article, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D2%AF%D1%81%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B9-%D0%BD%D0%B0%D0%B9%D1%80%D0%BD%D1%8B-%D1%85%D0%BE%D0%BE%D0%BB/)); cake.

### The biscuit plate: keep it small and odd

No haircut source sets a rule for the тавгийн идээ. The general Mongolian rules (from Tsagaan Sar sources) are: stack the biscuits in an **odd number of layers** (3, 5, 7 or 9); a grandfather's home uses 7, a father's home 5, and a young married couple's home 3; 9 layers are only for state events and very large weddings; and the top is decorated with cream and butter ([ikon.mn, 2014](https://ikon.mn/n/3vb); [ub.life, 2017](https://ub.life/p/6585)). So a young family's haircut feast fits **3 layers**. **[FB: personal experience]** That is what parents do: 3 layers of moulded biscuits cost about **60,000₮** (2026) from Барс market or the "Өгөөмж Архад" shop, and "elders said a huge plate isn't needed" ([group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2339810083485150/)). One family made 3 layers "out of respect, because two grandmothers were present" ([group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2342322679900557/)). Another made an all-ааруул plate for 178,000₮ ([group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2319611175505041/)).

### Ууц and dumplings divide families

No web source puts ууц on the haircut table. The traditional sheep at a haircut is a **gift** to the child, not a dish ([zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)). **[FB: personal experience]** Families split. The most popular post in the group (748 reactions) served ууц and sliced it for each guest ([group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2342322679900557/)). Another skipped it "because of our ethnic group's tradition" ([group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2339810083485150/)). In one Q&A, about 4 people said "it depends on the number of guests" and one said it is "pointless in the city" ([group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2346061212860037/)). One family's rule: a boy gets a full table with ууц, a girl does not. A lama told one mother: no biscuit plate and no ууц; serve light white foods and cake ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2306690880130404/)).

**[FB: personal experience]** "Closed food" (битүү хоол: бууз, хуушуур, банш) is debated in about 5 threads. Some say "not for a girl's ceremony" (because a girl has a "сав", a womb); some say "especially never for boys"; some say "avoid at any сэвлэг найр"; and one caterer family said "for a boy there SHOULD be closed food" ([group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2342322679900557/); [group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2346061212860037/); [group post, Oct 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2354257652040393/)). If your elders have no rule, the easy choice is to skip them.

### What parents actually served in 2026

**[FB: personal experience]** Typical menus: vegetable soup with мантуу buns, a fine meat stir-fry (нарийн махан хуурга), fried chicken for the children, seasoned rice, and 3 salads (the нийслэл potato salad is almost always one) ([group post, Oct 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2358849451581213/)). One family served хорхог (meat cooked with hot stones) and called it "a mistake": slow, "picnic food", and the sheep cost about 1,000,000₮ ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2295243264608499/)). Cake "is only for the table's look; no custom requires it", and one 10-inch cake is enough ([group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2339810083485150/)). Fruit cut small in cups or on sticks is popular; whole fruit gets wasted. One estimate: about 1 apple, 1 orange and a little watermelon per 6 people ([group post, Oct 2025](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2039412760191552/)).

**How much food?** No source gives amounts. Traditional writers say "олон зочин урихгүй" (do not invite many guests) ([arkhangai.mn, 2018](http://arkhangai.mn/index.php?newsid=3616)). **[FB: personal experience]** Over-ordering is the most common regret, in about 6 posts: "we planned 50, 35 came" ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2313831192749706/)); guests took the second course home ([group post, Oct 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2354257652040393/)). Small 200–300 ml drinks waste less. One host said disposable plates "felt uncomfortable; better to borrow plates" ([group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2339810083485150/)).

## Airag is welcome, vodka is not

The traditional rule is clear: "найранд архи дарс ордоггүй" (vodka and wine do not enter this feast). Airag is offered first, with a blessing ([arkhangai.mn, 2018](http://arkhangai.mn/index.php?newsid=3616)). The reason: the ceremony honours a small child's **four pure qualities**: no greed, no hoarding, no idea of "dirty", and no idea of high and low rank ([updown.mn, 2018](https://updown.mn/50592.html)). Airag, "a product of the herd", may be offered ([Huree.mn table article, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D0%B4%D0%B0%D0%B0%D1%85%D1%8C-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D0%BD%D0%B0%D0%B9%D1%80%D1%8B%D0%BD-%D1%88%D0%B8%D1%80%D1%8D%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D1%85/)). In about 2020, public figure Х.Хүрэлбаатар wrote on X: "Архи дарс амсаж хэрэглэхгүйг анхаар!" (be careful not to drink vodka or wine) ([X post](https://x.com/KhKhurelbaatar/status/1296622434720702464)). An opinion piece is titled "Хүүхдийн үсний найр томчуудын 'архидах'-ын шалтаг биш" (a child's hair feast is not an excuse for adults to drink) ([updown.mn, 2018](https://updown.mn/50592.html)).

Real life does not always follow the rule. A 2012 hotel banquet in Ulaanbaatar served "ample vodka, beer and juice" ([blog, 2012](https://livefromub.wordpress.com/2012/05/17/a-mongolian-hair-cutting-ceremony/)). One restaurant lets families bring their own drinks ([Huree.mn venues, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%82%D3%A9%D1%80%D1%81%D3%A9%D0%BD-%D3%A9%D0%B4%D3%A9%D1%80-%D2%AF%D1%81%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B9-%D0%BD%D0%B0%D0%B9%D1%80-%D1%85%D0%B8%D0%B9%D1%85%D1%8D/)). One long blessing text even praises arkhi (see Text 8 in the appendix). **[FB: personal experience]** In 2026, the parents agree more on this than on anything else. In one thread, all 7 commenters who spoke about it said no hard liquor, and about 5 said airag or "шим" (dairy drinks) is fine. One family planned Mongol vodka plus airag, a minority view ([group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2346061212860037/)). One family served no airag or vodka at all ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2308644699935022/)).

**[FB: personal experience] Amounts and prices.** One family bought 10 L of airag for about 40 guests and only 5 L was drunk ([group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2339810083485150/)); another bought 15 L ([group post, Oct 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2358849451581213/)). Sellers from Övörkhangai and Bulgan advertised airag at about **10,000₮ per litre, or 8,000₮ per litre for 15 L or more** (Aug–Sept 2026) ([group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2313831192749706/)).

## Comfort now matters as much as the deel

**Tradition.** On the morning of the day, the child is "dressed beautifully" ("гоё хувцаслан") and seated at the head of the table ([Huree.mn, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/)), "sometimes in a new deel" ([Mongolian Store](https://mongolianstore.com/tradition-of-first-hair-cutting-ceremony/)). The mother traditionally sews it ([Borderless Journal, 2025](https://borderlessjournal.com/2025/04/14/not-everyone-is-invited-to-a-childs-haircut-ceremony/)). In early times, a deel with patterns was taboo for small children ([Ergelt.mn, 2024](https://ergelt.mn/news/125/single/41227)), so a plain deel is the safe choice. **No source gives a colour rule for boys or girls**, and no source gives a dress code for parents or guests. One foreign guest wore a deel because the ceremony fell during the Tsagaan Sar holidays ([blog, 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html)).

**[FB: personal experience] The 2026 trend is comfort.** Parents say a full deel is hot and hard to keep on a small child, and has little later use. A thin Mongol shirt (монгол цамц) is worn longer. One child got angry and wanted to take the deel off; the parents bought matching Mongol shirts from "Хархорин" for 180,000₮ the pair ([group post, Sept 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2339810083485150/)). A grandmother sewed a reusable Mongol shirt ([group post, Oct 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2358849451581213/)). For one girl, a sister-in-law sewed a deel in the girl's "ивээл colour" (green), and the grandmother sewed the gift pouch and the hair pouch. The parents wore deels during the ritual and changed into Mongol shirts afterwards ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2295243264608499/)).

## Guests give money and a small gift; hosts thank the first cutter

### What guests give the child

**Traditionally**, guests "blessed the head" (толгойг мялааж) by promising livestock: a white sheep with a white face blaze (халзан цагаан хонь) or a lamb ([Huree.mn, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/); [zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn)). Some families held a small naadam the same day with foal races and wrestling ([24tsag.mn, 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755)). **Today** guests give toys, pencils and stationery, hair ribbons, badges, postcards, sweets and money ([Huree.mn, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/); [updown.mn, 2018](https://updown.mn/50592.html)). The gift is given **at the moment the guest cuts the hair**, together with the blessing ([24tsag.mn, 2020](https://www.24tsag.mn/a/185755)). Money is "a symbol of good fortune" ([blog, 2014](http://drvalfarmer.blogspot.com/2014/02/mongolian-haricutting-ceremony.html)). "The value of the gift matters less than the relationship it represents. Ask the parents when in doubt" ([Mongolian Store](https://mongolianstore.com/tradition-of-first-hair-cutting-ceremony/)). At one large party in 2014, gifts ranged from small toys to battery-powered cars and a bike ([blog, 2014](https://bransnmongolia.blogspot.com/2014/10/hair-cutting-ceremony.html)).

**[FB: personal experience]** A group explainer says guests put money **plus** soft ааруул or candy into the child's gift pouch (хишгийн уут): "never give only money" ([group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2274331730032986/)). One family complained that a weak pouch broke and "almost half the money got lost" ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2297606587705500/)). Buy a strong pouch and give one helper the job of keeping gifts safe.

**How much money?** **No reliable source gives a normal amount for 2025–2026.** A 2012 blog said "$5–20" ([blog, 2012](https://livefromub.wordpress.com/2012/05/17/a-mongolian-hair-cutting-ceremony/)), which is far too old to use. **[FB: personal experience]** Only one family gave totals: they spent 7.9 million₮ and received 5.5 million₮ from 55 adults and 40 children, plus toys, 2 pairs of shoes and clothes ([group post, 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2319611175505041/)). If only the adults gave money, that is roughly 100,000₮ per adult, but this is one family only.

### Хадаг colours and how to hand one over

Blue (цэнхэр) means the sky, eternity and peace; it is used between relatives and friends. White (цагаан) means a pure mind; it is a very high honour, for Buddhas and the most honoured elders. Yellow is for one's religious teacher only. Green and red are for special Buddhist rites ([eguur.mn](https://eguur.mn/656176/)). To present a хадаг, fold it lengthwise three times, hold it with the open side facing the receiver, and the receiver takes it with both hands ([Mongolian Wikipedia, "Хадаг"](https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A5%D0%B0%D0%B4%D0%B0%D0%B3)). Use blue for the scissors and the hair. A white хадаг suits a thank-you to the first cutter. No source says guests must bring a хадаг to this ceremony.

### What hosts give back

Written sources say little. Hosts feed all the guests and give each visiting child a bowl of цагаалга ([updown.mn, 2018](https://updown.mn/50592.html)). If an uninvited person happens to arrive, it is a lucky sign: invite them in, seat them high, feed them, and send them off with a gift ([mglnews.mn](http://www.mglnews.mn/content/read/58281.htm)).

## Guests say short blessings; the reciter says the long one

Every person who cuts the hair says a blessing (ерөөл). Ordinary guests use a short formula. The first cutter can say a little more. A hired ерөөлч or a gifted elder recites the long verse. Full long texts are in the appendix.

**Short formulas for guests:**

> Урт наслан, удаан жарга
>
> Урт настай, удаан жаргалтай, аавдаа ачтай, ээждээ тустай, төр улсдаа зүтгэлтэй, түмэн олны манлай болж яваарай

Meaning: "Live long, be happy for a long time. Be long-lived and happy, a help to your father and mother, devoted to your country, and a leader among the people." Traditional; said while the child tastes milk after the first cut ([zindaa.mn, 2018](https://news.zindaa.mn/2adn); [Huree.mn, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/); [updown.mn, 2018](https://updown.mn/55761.html), which writes "Урт насалж, удаан жарга").

> урт насалж удаан жаргаарай, аав ээждээ ачтай, ахан дүүсдээ тустай, төрдөө үнэнч, түмэндээ хүндтэй сайн хүн болоорой

Meaning: "Live long and be happy; be grateful to your parents, helpful to your brothers and sisters, loyal to the state, and a good person respected by all." Traditional ([updown.mn, 2019](https://updown.mn/80564.html)).

> аавдаа ачлалтай, ээждээ энэрэлтэй, урт насалж, удаан жаргаж элбэг баян амьдраарай

Meaning: "Be grateful to your father, kind to your mother, live long, be happy long, and live in plenty." Traditional ([updown.mn, 2018](https://updown.mn/50592.html)).

> Том хүн болоорой! / Сайн хүн болоорой!

Meaning: "Grow into a big person! / Become a good person!" Heard at a ceremony in Ulaanbaatar ([blog, 2012](https://livefromub.wordpress.com/2012/05/17/a-mongolian-hair-cutting-ceremony/)).

**Tips for guests.** **[FB: personal experience]** One explainer says each guest should prepare their own blessing and not repeat the others, because "words have power" ([group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2274331730032986/)). A mother who cut first regretted saying only "сайн хүн болоорой" instead of a proper blessing ([group post, Aug 2026](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2295243264608499/)). An English guide advises a short, sincere blessing rather than copying words you do not understand ([Mongolian Store](https://mongolianstore.com/tradition-of-first-hair-cutting-ceremony/)). The website Creative Mongolia suggests printing a list of short wishes for guests to read (Text 9 in the appendix) ([Creative Mongolia](https://cm.nmma.co/135444/)).

## Good manners for hosts and guests

**For hosts.**

- Invite with care: siblings, relatives, respected people, and the ивээл person ([Huree.mn, 2024](https://huree.mn/%D1%85%D2%AF%D2%AF%D1%85%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%B9%D0%BD-%D1%81%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%B3-%D2%AF%D1%80%D0%B3%D1%8D%D1%8D%D1%85-%D1%91%D1%81%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BB/)). Traditional writers prefer people with "complete" families (үр хүүхэд нь өв тэгш хүмүүс) ([updown.mn, 2018](https://updown.mn/50592.html)).
- Start before noon. Seat the honoured guest at the north-west, or at the head table.
- No quarrels or angry words that day ("ам муруйж болдоггүй"). No hard alcohol. Fresh food only ([updown.mn, 2018](https://updown.mn/50592.html)).
- Lead the child sunwise.
- Give each person their own small milk cup **[FB]**. Brief the MC on the cutting order **[FB]**.
- Welcome any unexpected visitor as good luck ([mglnews.mn](http://www.mglnews.mn/content/read/58281.htm)).
- **[FB: personal experience]** Do not exhaust yourself. "I cooked myself last year, never sat for a moment, and regretted it" ([group post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2342322679900557/)). "It's your child's and your celebration; do what suits you" ([group post, Oct 2025](https://www.facebook.com/groups/288381221961390/posts/2039412760191552/)).

**For guests.**

- Arrive before the cutting starts, and before noon ([updown.mn, 2018](https://updown.mn/50592.html)).
- Wait to be called. Cut only a small lock.
- Sip milk, say a short, sincere blessing, and give your gift at that moment.
- Do not take any hair or joke about it without permission. Ask the parents if you are unsure what to give ([Mongolian Store](https://mongolianstore.com/tradition-of-first-hair-cutting-ceremony/)).
- Avoid arguments and drinking during the rite.

### Criticism of the growing cost

Writers say the feast has become "хавтгайруулсан нийтийн найр" (a sprawling public party) with all relatives, friends and coworkers ([arkhangai.mn, 2018](http://arkhangai.mn/index.php?newsid=3616)). **Who cuts first is the clearest difference.** There are four patterns: an elder or grandfather (Khalkh and most western groups in Mongolia); the **mother's brother** (Kalmyk, Tunka Buryat, Tuvans of China); the **midwife** (Buryat in older Mongolian sources); and an **elder with a matching zodiac** (Deed Mongols, Inner Mongolia). The Buryat sources conflict. The Mongolian phrase "эх барьж авсан ... эх" most likely means the midwife. Some English texts translate it as "the birth mother", which is probably a mistranslation ([mongolmom, 2016](https://mongolmom.blogspot.com/2016/04/mongolian-childrens-first-hair-cutting.html); [Burunen.ru, 2025](https://burunen.ru/news/society/136427-v-tunke-vozrozhdayut-iskonnyy-obychay-strizhki-volos/)).

**The odd/even rule seems to belong to central (Khalkh) practice.** Groups farther away mostly use one age for everyone (1 or 3) or a local age. **What happens to the hair also differs a lot.** For example, the old Tunka Buryat custom of leaving hair in a tree for birds is a good deed there, but birds' nests are the main fear in the Khalkh taboo.

**Kazakh families in Bayan-Ölgii** most likely follow the Kazakh 40-day custom, not the Mongol ceremony at 2–5 years. No Bayan-Ölgii source confirms this, and no source covers mixed Kazakh–Mongol families ([kznews.kz, 2018](https://kznews.kz/article/qyrqynan-shygaru-balanyn-shashyn-alu)). Islamic teaching quoted there forbids cutting only part of the hair and leaving the rest, which is the opposite of the Mongol tuft. Islam itself recommends the 7th day and sees the 40-day rite as a permitted local custom ([islam.kz](https://islam.kz/kk/articles/atamura/balany-qyrqynan-shygaru-dasturi-jane-onyn-dindegi-orny-2938/)).

**Religion changes who sets the date and where the hair goes, not the steps.** Lamas choose the date among Khalkh, Tunka Buryat, Deed Mongol and Kalmyk families. Kalmyks keep the hair near Buddha images ([baylig.ru, 2022](https://baylig.ru/nasledie/kak-obmanut-shulmusov/)). No source describes a separate shamanist family's version step by step. **The move to the city is the same everywhere:** from ger to hall, from livestock to money, from close kin to a wide social circle. The Tunka Buryats now gather in a hall ([Burunen.ru, 2025](https://burunen.ru/news/society/136427-v-tunke-vozrozhdayut-iskonnyy-obychay-strizhki-volos/)), and one Deed Mongol family held its 2021 ceremony in the city of Xining ([ihchina.cn, 2023](https://www.ihchina.cn/luntan_details/27930.html)).

## Where sources disagree, and how to decide

**A simple rule for every disagreement:**

1. **What your own family did before wins.** Ask how the parents' and older siblings' ceremonies were done. Many families repeat the same custom for every child.
2. **For dates, years and the first cutter, ask your family's usual lama or astrologer.** Choose one lama. Do not mix advice from several lamas. Ask "why?" so you understand the reason.
3. **For everything else, use the "most common version" in this guide.**

## Questions to ask your elders before you plan

Many regional and family customs are not written down anywhere online. Your elders are the only source for them. Ask both the father's side and the mother's side, because their customs may differ.

**Family background**

1. Which ethnic group and home region does each side of our family come from? Which side's custom will we follow?
2. Do we have a family lama or astrologer we always ask?

**Age and date**

3. At what age were the parents' and the older siblings' hair taken? Was it counted in хий нас or birthday age?
4. Do girls and boys in our family follow the odd/even rule, or something else?
5. Is there a season, month or weekday our family always avoids (for example Thursday)?
6. Should younger siblings have it at the same age as the eldest, or at the same time?

**First cutter and guests**

7. Who cut first at earlier ceremonies: an ивээл-year person, the grandfather, the mother's brother, the midwife?
8. Must the first cutter be a man? Married? A certain age?
9. Which relatives must be there? Must the авга and нагац come?
10. Is there anyone who should not touch the child's hair?
11. If an important relative cannot come, do we leave a lock for them?

**The ritual**

12. Do we use a wooden knife? Who makes it?
13. Do we make a сан (incense) offering or a цацал (milk sprinkling) first?
14. Which side do we start on? Do we go sunwise or by age?
15. Do we leave any tufts (тэвэг, хөхөл, сонжуу, согсоо)? Do we fully shave, or only trim a girl's hair?
16. Who does the final shave, and when?

**The hair**

17. Where do we keep the hair? Do we put anything in the pouch with it (for example rice or juniper)?
18. Do we give it to the child later? At what age?

**Food, drinks and gifts**

19. What must be on our table? How do we make цагаалга? How many layers of biscuits?
20. Do we serve ууц? Do we avoid closed dumplings (бууз, хуушуур, банш) for a boy or a girl?
21. Do we allow only airag, or no alcohol at all?
22. What do we give the first cutter (fabric, milk, a хадаг, money, a sheep)?
23. Do we promise the child livestock?

**Blessings**

24. Is there a family blessing or song? Who in the family can recite one?

Write the answers down. They become your family's own rulebook for the next child.

## Conclusion

The fixed core of this ceremony is small: a good morning, a respected first cutter, хадаг-tied scissors, milk, a blessing and gift from each guest, and hair kept for life. Almost every online argument, about even or odd ages, ууц, dumplings or which day, sits outside that core. Many of these arguments come from counting age in different ways, from different lamas, or from one family's habit becoming a "rule". So the real risk for a host is not breaking a sacred rule. It is ordinary planning trouble: too much food, a late invitation, an MC who calls the wrong people up, or a parent too tired to enjoy the day. The Facebook reports show this clearly.

There is also a shift in who carries the tradition. In the past, a respected elder knew the words and led the rite. Today a hired ерөөлч and fiddle player often fill that role, and the old regional versions (Buryat, Oirat, Darkhad and others) are mostly unwritten. That makes the questions you ask your elders more valuable than any website. If you write down your family's answers, and what you do on the day, you create the record that the internet does not have.

Cutting from the back of the head gives him great strength. May he be wise in books, alert in sleep, skilled in work, eloquent in speech, fair in law, brave in war, and a swimmer at sea. May he live in peace with his family and homeland, with so many children and young horses that he cannot tell them apart, with good fortune, learning, festivals and joy, a long life and big feasts. I offer this blessing."
